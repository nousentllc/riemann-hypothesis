# Allocating 27 recording values

The existing experiment measured 4,992 licensed decisions out of 5,184 and zero true positives for three retained residue digits. We now test a mathematical design prediction on that same disclosed population. Include all 81 residues with equal weight and all 64 positive integer questions with equal weight. For a deterministic record, license a yes or no exactly when every state consistent with it gives that answer. Uniformity here defines a benchmark, not a model of classical choice.

Compare the three-digit record with an allocation that puts all empty cases together, isolates 11 singleton profiles, and groups the remaining profiles into seven triples and eight quadruples. Before running the comparison, arithmetic predicts 191 undetermined pairs versus 192, with 11 positives recovered rather than zero. Also test whether 12 positives are possible at the old coverage with at most 27 values. Treat the proposed design as a predicted construction for a known dataset. Verify the optimum over tag partitions, check the optimizer by independent brute force on smaller examples, and count individual decisions lost as well as gained. Compare success under a permutation of the positive labels. Do not generalize to any empirical memory system.
