# Why the arithmetic window uses two nonzero theta lifts

**A proof trace of the definite-unitary stable-range argument in the published Lean source**  
Nous research notes · 23 November 2026 · Source commit `40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005`

For an even input rank, the arithmetic argument goes up to a larger odd rank twice. The odd-rank theorem can then be applied to each lift. Comparing the answers lets the proof remove the extra characters and recover Galois data in the original rank. This construction would fail if a theta lift produced the zero function. We traced how the source rules out exactly that failure, and why the resulting nonzero vector has the additional properties that its caller needs. [Even and odd branches](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/CoreAtQIffRowQ.lean#L215-L220) · [Two applications to the same rank](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/TwoUpOKOfSteps.lean#L13-L33)

Two outcomes emerged from reading the proof bodies. First, a strict inequality between a core integral and its tail is sharp under the hypotheses of the noncancellation lemma. Lean accepted both the original argument and a counterexample to changing its last inequality to a weak one. Second, the theorem promises a sufficiently large dimension without naming it. Its counting proof contains enough information to reconstruct a conservative sufficient dimension and then reduce it by two. The sharpness of the integral inequality and the sufficiency of the dimension estimate have different meanings. Nothing here determines the first dimension at which a particular lift is nonzero.

## 1. What the window actually consumes

The window at this point in the argument controls arithmetic cohomology. Fix a character, a sufficiently small positive ε, and a function choosing admissible exponents. The theorem supplies J≥1 and a lower cutoff. At each sufficiently large X and each prime pair satisfying `WinPair` at X^(1/J), it gives Y with X^(1/J)≤Y and Y²≤X. On **every** x in [Y,Y²], the relevant cohomological lengths are finite through the specified range, and a sum of lengths weighted by log primes is bounded by √exp(x^ε), with integer cutoff ⌊x^401⌋. The prime congruences, sizes and divisibility conditions are part of `WinPair`. Subsequent arguments are needed to reach a prime-sum window. [Definitions of the lengths and window](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean#L12-L49)

There is a way to check whether the theta theorem does mathematical work here. Follow the datum until a proof *uses a component of it*. `RowSrc` supplies the local–global Galois theorem by passing `tLiftK` into a construction that also requires an independent geometric input. `Development` sends the lift into its core construction. In `allCoreQ_of` the odd branch calls the given odd-rank result directly, while the even branch calls `hSub` with the two lifts. At the other end, `vwin_of_row` supplies the Galois row to the window construction. Its literature record carries the row in `litN.aut.gal`. The rank-three unitary argument projects precisely that field and passes it into weight-compatible descent and adjoint automorphy. The window proof then uses a unitary endpoint for count V alongside counts I, II and III and its separate lemma K. This identifies an actual route of use in the published proof. It does not establish logical indispensability across all proofs. ## 2. The promise made before the target is known

Let E be the CM field, F its maximal real subfield, n≥1 and X the definite source Hermitian space of rank n. The base datum must satisfy `GoodK` and the source Gram matrix is the identity. At this stage `TLiftK` supplies d₀. **Only then** are d≥d₀, the definite skew-Hermitian target with its specified scalar Gram matrix, the two Hecke characters, infinity exponents, source representation U and weight λ supplied. For each real place w and each source coordinate k there is the condition

$$d + m_W(w) \leq 2\lambda_w(k).$$

Increasing the target dimension while holding the weight and the character fixed can therefore invalidate an application. In the even-rank caller, the proof takes a common lower bound on the finitely many source weights *before* selecting characters with compatible exponents. This is how it meets the inequality at the dimension it has chosen. [Full quantifiers](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/HalfIndexLattices/TLiftK.lean#L12-L23) · [Construction of the two choices](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/TwoUpOKOfSteps.lean#L13-L33)

The conclusion is an automorphic constituent U′ with prescribed weights and split-place matching. On the first n weight coordinates the claimed rule is 2λ′ₖ=2λₖ−d−m_W+n+m_X. On the remaining coordinates it is 2λ′ₖ=n+m_X. At a split place the matching conclusion applies when the respective local components occur. The conclusion supplies one constituent with these properties; it gives no canonical choice of constituent. [Definition of the lift](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/SchurStep.lean#L103-L112)

In the actual construction, `realPair` supplies an archimedean polynomial f₀ and a nonzero automorphic vector φ of the same compact type. The nonvanishing argument chooses a finite part φ_f **for those very vectors**. Their theta integral is a nonzero automorphic form. The projection theorem supplies an equivariant projection to some irreducible automorphic U′ whose image of this form is nonzero. The proof uses that same projection and that same vector to read both the weight and the matching local components. Merely finding a nonzero function somewhere and an unrelated constituent with suitable weights would leave a genuine gap in this reasoning. ## 3. The inequality that prevents cancellation

There are two integrals in play. Θ is the theta lift, a function on the target. I is a scalar on the source group G, defined by integrating a coefficient of the Weil representation against the conjugate of the matrix coefficient of φ. For the chosen model and test datum Φ, the proof establishes both Θ≡0 ⇒ I=0 and I≠0. It is the combination that forces a nonzero automorphic form. A coefficient being nonzero at the identity gives neither statement by itself. [The two uses in a single proof body](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGNonVan.lean#L116-L151)

To force I≠0, the proof first selects finite places S covering the level needed to fix φ and the ramification needed for the character. At S it uses local test functions supported on small subgroups. Outside S it uses vacuum vectors. Write A for the archimedean group, B for the group at S, and C for the remaining finite part. The support in B is a positive-measure subgroup H that fixes the source vector. On A, the matching compact representation allows Schur orthogonality to replace an integral by a nonzero scalar. Its normalization is μ_A(A)/dim(V), with V finite-dimensional and nonzero. There is also a nonzero Weil coefficient at the identity. Up to nonzero factors, one is left with an integral over C. [Choice of data](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGNonVan.lean#L39-L87) · [Schur scalar](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGSchurStep.lean#L193-L212) · [Assembly with product Haar measure](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGNonVan.lean#L116-L151)

Write the remaining integrand as s(c)c₀(c), its integral subgroup as K, and r for the source matrix coefficient at the identity. The proof knows r>0. On K it obtains s=1 and c₀=r exactly. Across C it has |c₀|≤r and |s|≤w, where w is controlled by the height to the power d/2. Consequently

$$
\left|\int_K sc_0\right|=r\mu_C(K),\qquad
\left|\int_{C\setminus K}sc_0\right|\leq r\int_{C\setminus K}w
< r\mu_C(K).
$$

No choice of complex phases outside K can cancel the contribution on K. The proof thereby obtains a nonzero total integral without controlling the sign of the whole integrand. The margin may depend on the data. The theorem supplies no uniform numerical lower bound for it. [Original abstract proof](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/BlockDeterminants/AbsNe.lean#L31-L64) · [Verification of the automorphic hypotheses](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGNonVan.lean#L130-L154)

**A sharp control.** Take A and B to have one point of mass one. Give C two atoms of mass one, put the first in K, and set H=B. Make all scalar factors and the weight equal to one, and take s=+1 on K and s=−1 on the other atom. The core and tail have the same absolute size and the total is zero. Our Lean proof changes only the last `<` of the original universal statement to `≤` and proves the negation of that modified statement. This is sharpness over all data allowed by the abstract hypotheses. An automorphic datum can still give a nonzero total when a coarse upper bound on its tail happens to equal its core. [Exact original hypothesis](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/SchurStep.lean#L183-L189) · [Formal counterexample](verification/WeakTail.lean)

**How S is selected.** On the full group let U_int be the elements integral at every place. It is open, contains the identity and has positive Haar mass. Let m have density equal to the height power with respect to Haar measure. Integrability gives m(G)<∞. For a finite S let A_S be those g integral at every place in S but outside U_int. These sets decrease. Their intersection is empty because each g outside U_int has a place witnessing failure of integrality. Continuity from above makes m(A_S) tend to zero. Consequently S can contain any initially prescribed set S₀ and still satisfy m(A_S)<μ(U_int). Product decomposition and invariance transport this to the strict comparison on C. The positive finite normalization factors cancel. The proof gives no rate for the decrease of m(A_S) and identifies no specific list of places. ## 4. Why a zero theta lift would kill the scalar

Let z₀=(d−n)/2. The doubling argument varies the scalar integral in a complex parameter z by inserting a power a(g)^(z−z₀) of a positive height. At z=z₀ that factor becomes one. A domination estimate with the power d/2 and the uniform bound on the matrix coefficient give holomorphy in Re z>t₀−n/2. The proof controls a neighborhood of each point in that half-plane. **At the special point the strict interior condition is exactly d/2>t₀.** This is one mathematical job performed by the large target dimension. The other is the geometric stable range d≥2n, used for local test functions. [Holomorphy and domination](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/TPGRallis.lean#L140-L185) · [Where the two rank requirements enter](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/GalTPDefs.lean#L141-L163)

Far to the right the doubled Eisenstein pairing P(z) unfolds to c c′ I(z), where the comparison constants are nonzero. The difference continues as zero by the identity theorem on the relevant regular domain. This provides P(z₀)=c c′ I(z₀). At the same point the anisotropic Siegel–Weil value identifies the Eisenstein expression with a theta average. Fubini and conjugation reorganize it as a normalization times an integral of a squared absolute value. In the stable range n<d, the special-value factor in the source is 1. If the theta lift vanishes as a function, the inner integral vanishes for every target element. Thus P(z₀)=0 and I(z₀)=0, contradicting Section 3. This explains why a bound on an auxiliary scalar can decide a question about a function on a different group. ## 5. Recovering a numerical sufficient dimension

The public statement hides d₀ behind an existential. The proof supplies enough structure to give it a value on paper. Write D=[F:ℚ]. From the matrix count one obtains a finite constant C and a growth base b for the dyadic level sets of the height a,

$$\mu\{g:a(g)\geq 2^{-j}\}\leq Cb^j.$$

Only the exponential rate affects integrability. A compact set, a shift of the dyadic index, and the normalization of Haar measure affect the prefactor. The source counts both a matrix and its inverse, but its simultaneous covering estimate raises the covering number to a **single** coordinate count q. The matrix is n×n over the adelic extension of degree two, so q=2n² over the adeles of F. [Which matrix is counted](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/ThkBaseHgtInt.lean#L32-L47) · [Simultaneous count](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/LatticeBasics/HgtCount.lean#L77-L82) · [Rank two after base change](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/SiegelWeilAstepCLawB.lean#L20-L24)

The counting proof chooses a scaling unit of norm 1/2 at all infinite places and equal to one at finite places. Its **inverse** has adelic Haar modulus Δ=2^D. This uses the infinite-place multiplicities of F and the unit finite factors. The quadratic extension E/F has already contributed to q. **Can the estimate alone do better?** Consider the nonnegative integers j. Put mass b^j at j and define f(j)=2^(−j). Its ℓ-th superlevel set has mass (b^(ℓ+1)−1)/(b−1) ≤ [b/(b−1)] b^ℓ. At t=α each term is one, and at t<α the terms grow. Thus the count *by itself* cannot force integrability at or below α. This is a written counterexample on a discrete measure space. It establishes a limit on inference from the count, while leaving the integrability of the actual adelic height at that endpoint undecided.

Neither value gives a cutoff X for the arithmetic window.

## 6. Why one lift would leave the comparison underdetermined

For even n form two lifts U₁ and U₂ to the same odd rank n+t. Their character data include a common twist κ and two families of added characters with no character in common across the families. The proof applies the odd-rank result twice and obtains Galois representations r₁ and r₂. At suitable split Frobenius classes the two Satake multisets share the same twisted base contribution. If Lᵢ(g) denotes the product of the linear factors from the i-th auxiliary family, density and continuity extend the identity

$$\mathrm{cp}(r_1,g)L_2(g)=\mathrm{cp}(r_2,g)L_1(g)$$

to every g. Disjointness is a statement about entire characters as functions of g. All characters agree at the identity. Requiring distinct values at every g would make even the simplest examples impossible. [How the two Satake identities and density enter](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/CoreAtQIffRowQ.lean#L103-L145)

The equality compares the semisimple sums r₁⊕diag(L₂) and r₂⊕diag(L₁). Brauer–Nesbitt gives an intertwiner and transfers an eigenline for a character of L₁ to the first sum. Cross-family disjointness forces a nonzero projection of that eigenvector to r₁. Semisimplicity supplies an invariant complement, so one can split off the line and cancel a linear polynomial factor. Repeating this step recovers a rank-n representation. Twisting it back restores the base local data. The argument can construct a representative at a particular good place, then uses isomorphism and conjugacy to transfer local compatibility to any weak representative.
