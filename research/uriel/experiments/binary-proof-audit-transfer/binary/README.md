# Why the two binary mass limits agree

A binary Hermitian space supplies a four-coordinate count over its ground field and a one-coordinate count of lengths. At finite scale the source equates the first to the second multiplied by an initially unknown coefficient κ. To recover κ, the author proves that each count approaches the same finite, nonzero number. The proof of equality of the two limits uses the **same test function, the same local sets, and the same coordinate Haar data in the limiting volume**. At finite scale the left integral uses a normalized theta measure and the right integrals use Gram-fiber measures. An explicit closed form for their shared mass never enters the argument. [OneVecBinary, 15–27](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/SiegelWeilProof/BinaryMassMeans/OneVecBinary.lean#L15-L27) [CountingFormula, 91–99](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/CountingFormula.lean#L91-L99) [CountingFormula, 125–145](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/CountingFormula.lean#L125-L145)

## The witness and its order

Write D = [F:ℚ]. Here E/F is the quadratic CM extension, J is the anisotropic nondegenerate Hermitian form on two E-coordinates, and one row X consists of two entries in E. The spaces of vectors at infinity and at v have coordinate Haar measures W∞ and Wᵥ built from the same basis b of E/F and base Haar measures ℓ∞ and λᵥ. The ring of local integers has λᵥ-volume one, its coordinate vector lattice has Wᵥ-volume one, and an archimedean fundamental domain for the integers of F has ℓ∞-volume one. A test consists of a nonnegative continuous compactly supported function φ, its support in the open locus of unit Gram determinant, and compact-open sets Aᵥ in that locus at the places of a finite S. Away from S the source uses the standard lattice. In this notation the Lean definition reads V = (∫⁻ X, ENNReal.ofReal (φ X) ∂W∞) · ∏ᵥ∈S Wᵥ(Aᵥ). The ordinary integral of φ gives the same finite archimedean mass after conversion because φ is nonnegative and integrable. [MeanVal, 67–88](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/SiegelWeilProof/BinaryMassMeans/MeanVal.lean#L67-L88) [IsEulerProd, 73–86](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/CompanionTheory/SpectralGeometry/CountingDefinitions/AdelicEulerProducts/IsEulerProd.lean#L73-L86)

At each selected finite place a nonempty compact-open neighborhood supplies a positive finite factor. ## The left side counts the original vectors

For a fixed unitary translate h, flattening one row with two E-entries yields four F-coordinates, hence 4D real dimensions. Dilating the archimedean entries by ε multiplies lengths by ε². The count itself receives the vector normalization ε^(4D). The source moves the translate into the test and, when necessary, enlarges the finite set of places to handle its coordinates. The unitary change of variables preserves the coordinate measures. Standard lattice factors inserted at the extra places have mass one. The equidistribution theorem therefore sends the normalized translated count to V independent of h. Removing the zero vector is legitimate because the support condition gives φ(0) = 0. ## The right side counts the lengths

The base quantity β is a nonzero element of F. A one-by-one Hermitian matrix has the form of an embedded scalar. On the base-changed quadratic algebra its inverse coordinate is half the algebra trace of the sole matrix entry. The trace of an embedded scalar is twice that scalar. This inverse and the induced measure identity fix the base coordinate in the density integral. Finite and infinite places use compatible coordinate Haar measures. [ArchimedeanBinaryGramDensity, 23–50](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/ArchimedeanBinaryGramDensity.lean#L23-L50) [ArchimedeanBinaryGramDensity, 72–131](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/ArchimedeanBinaryGramDensity.lean#L72-L131)

For a scalar length s let G(s) be the integral of φ on the archimedean Gram fiber over s, and gᵥ(s) the mass of Aᵥ on its local fiber, converted to a real number. The limit at scale δ of the normalized weighted count compares the product over all places to a product truncated to a finite set. For all but finitely many places, of residue size q, the original bound chooses C = 3. For nonzero s outside the local integers the density vanishes. For nonzero integral s the error from one is at most 3/q and on units it is at most 3/q². At a good place its integral over the base is one. The individual value at a particular β may still differ from one. The weaker 1/q estimate at places dividing β cannot be summed uniformly as though every β were a unit. Counting β in ideal lattices supplies an extra inverse ideal norm. The proof bounds the truncation error by expanding products over subsets and controlling the resulting sum, then applies equidistribution to the finite truncation. ## Closing the argument and testing where it can fail

Anisotropy rules out nonzero vectors of zero length. The proof reindexes all nonzero rational vectors by β ≠ 0 and applies the supplied Euler product identity separately on every sphere. Its quantifier requires **one κ for all nonzero β**. The source obtains Lε = κ Rε for each ε, then uniqueness of limits gives V = κV. The two hypotheses to cancellation in ℝ≥0∞ are V ≠ 0 and V ≠ ∞. The existence theorem supplied them for the common test. If κ could vary with β, one could deduce a weighted mean, and the same division would fail to identify each coefficient. If two different functions were used, multiplying the right function by two would multiply its limiting mass by two. If both sides used the zero test, the equation would hold for every finite κ. These controls pinpoint three independently necessary links. [CountingFormula, 41–86](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/CountingFormula.lean#L41-L86) [CountingFormula, 125–145](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/CountingFormula.lean#L125-L145)

Where does a *single* κ come from? In the enclosing proof, proportionality supplies some c for the target sphere. If one of the relevant fiber measures is zero, the theta measure vanishes and coefficient one is already allowed. Otherwise the Hasse input makes the target rational sphere nonempty. For another represented length, similitude invariance transports the same c. For an unrepresented length, the Hasse input forces the archimedean fiber measure to vanish, the global sphere is empty, and the zero-measure identity accepts that same c. This gives the universal hypothesis for the counting theorem. One cannot infer proportionality at each β with an unrelated choice of c and then factor out a common coefficient. [VectorLengthFormulaOfParts, 206–244](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/VectorLengthFormulaOfParts.lean#L206-L244) [OneVectorFormulaOfParts, 11–40](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/BlockDecomposition/OneVectorFormulaOfParts.lean#L11-L40)

## A finite computation with an evaluable common mass

Take the real coordinates of ℚ(i)² with the ordinary positive Hermitian form and define f(s) = max(0,1 − |2s−3|), φ(X) = f(|X|²). Its support is the closed shell 1 ≤ |X|² ≤ 2. In four real dimensions the area of the unit three-sphere is 2π². Substituting s = r² changes the volume element to π² s ds. Symmetry about 3/2 and the triangle area 1/2 give the exact integral 3π²/4, approximately 7.40220330.

For ε = 1/k the first computation enumerates the weighted integer points by squared norm, independently cross-checking two decompositions of four squares. The second samples the **real** density π²s f(s) on the length mesh δ = 1/k². The grid contains both endpoints and for even k it contains the apex as well. Reflection about 3/2 makes the linear factor s average exactly 3/2, and the trapezoid areas for f are exact. For odd k the apex is halfway between two sample points and the missing triangle has area 1/(2k⁴). The length-mesh sum then equals (3π²/4)(1−k⁻⁴).

| k | Normalized vector sum | Real length-mesh sum divided by π² |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 19/2 | 3/4 |
| 3 | 5216/729 | 20/27 |
| 32 | 496750959/67108864 | 3/4 |

At k = 1 both discrete counts miss an entire shell with positive mass. At k = 2 the length mesh already gives the exact integral and the vector sum overshoots it by about 2.098. At k = 32 the vector sum is about 0.00003719 below the integral. These are respectively a **single lattice** and a **length sum with only real fiber density**. The original equality at every scale involves an average over a unitary quotient and finite-place weights. Only the original proof supplies their identification. Run “python3 radial_control.py” for all ten exact comparisons and the independent counting check. The example computes no exceptional prime, φ, A, or V selected by the general existence theorem.

A coordinate substitution makes a particularly revealing negative control. With the raw trace t = 2s, integrating the density π²(t/2)f(t/2) against dt requires the factor 1/2. Dropping that Jacobian doubles the mass to 3π²/2. In this example every t-grid of mesh 1/k² hits both shell endpoints and the apex t = 3. Reflection makes the sampled sum exactly 3π²/2 on **all ten grids** when the Jacobian is omitted, and exactly 3π²/4 when restored. This produces a stable, apparently exact factor of two with a completely known cause. The original scalar coordinate qR incorporates half the trace, so the published base measure does not incur this spurious extra factor. This control concerns the rank-one Hermitian coordinate; the separate threshold n ≥ d in the global Siegel–Weil constant has a different proof.

## A finite control for the prime-divisor gain

For a prime q define an artificial nonnegative local density 1 + dᵩ. On multiples of q let dᵩ = (q−1)/q² and on the other residues let dᵩ = −1/q². Its mean over a period is exactly one because the exceptional multiples occupy one residue class in q. Its mean absolute deviation is 2(q−1)/q³. Thus an error as large as order 1/q at a *selected length* contributes only order 1/q² to the average. The absolute values prevent signed cancellation from explaining this gain. For example at q = 5 the exceptional deviation is 4/25 while the mean absolute deviation is 8/125.

For a set of distinct primes the Chinese remainder theorem makes the residue coordinates independent over one full period. Consequently the mean product of absolute deviations is the product of those absolute means. Conditioning on lengths divisible by every prime increases the statistic. At q = 5 alone it grows by a factor 5/2. This toy model makes visible the extra inverse norm in the published estimate. The real argument has a dilating compact archimedean window and an arbitrary number field. Exact periodic independence is available only in the finite control. Run “python3 density_control.py” for six exact cases, including a simultaneous-period test at 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11.
