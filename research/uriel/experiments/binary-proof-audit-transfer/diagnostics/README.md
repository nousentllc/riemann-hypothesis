# Can a shortcut pick the measurement?

This prospective diagnostic checks how much of the proposed transfer test can be solved by regularities in the packet construction. The protocol and findings were prepared without inspecting model responses. The audit differentiates choosing a measurement from the harder task of producing an answer with a valid certificate after seeing it. The tables below grant *perfect* reading and certificate construction after each fixed action.

## Original proposed panel, before inference

The six formal and six circuit families give 24 distinct records. In both domains four records are settled before lookup, four require a measurement, and four remain open under all measurements. The strongest final targets comprise four yes, four no and four unknown per domain. In each pair exactly one observed reading changes. Public descriptions, candidate catalog, lookup menus and output grammars are identical across its two versions. Replacing the hidden state, accepted truth labels, hidden outcomes and admissible-set list with sentinel values leaves the rendered initial prompt and output grammar unchanged while preserving the public candidate labels. The administrator constructs each arm’s request using only its system instruction, the same rendered record and the same per-record seed. A follow-up discloses only the selected observation. This check addresses explicit transmission through the observed interface and does not rule out every semantic shortcut.

On **all four of the first proposed formal lookup cases**, the useful option was the only nonconstant measurement. Choosing a varying column does not require checking which mathematical alternatives it separates. Three of those four options were in the last displayed position. This weakens any inference about selecting *discriminating* evidence on the first formal layout. It does not give a model the answer or its required certificate. This finding was reported to the lead researcher before the holdout was administered and prompted a scored-only redesign. The legacy generator and this first audit are retained to make that change visible.

The circuit lookup items always have multiple varying measurement columns. A truth-blind most-distinct or most-balanced rule with fixed display-order ties resolves only one of the four cases requiring a measurement. No fixed position solves more than two. In both domains the kind of record is identical over **every** state compatible with the initial evidence. A productive measurement resolves the claim for either possible reply. The optimum therefore never relies on peeking at a hidden actual state.

Inference with weak clues can look better if the certificate is discarded. The modal sign among eligible states, abstaining on ties, predicts 9/12 final *labels* in each domain. A constant sign or constant unknown predicts 4/12. Such a majority rule makes unsupported assertions. These label counts give it no evidence-certificate credit. Additional label-only strategies and leave-one-family-out diagnostics appear in the machine-readable records. The latter give tiny descriptive matched samples; one cannot estimate the performance of an independently learned shortcut from six families per domain.

## Interpretation

The six formal families represent different mathematical propositions. The six circuits have structurally distinct physical layouts, and independent circuit solvers established the target values. Across both domains, however, agents receive an exhaustive table of possible cases, one visible fact and a menu of three further observations. A successful model still might be transferring a finite filtering routine across subject matter. The study cannot directly establish open-ended research ability, source navigation, autonomous experiment design or step-by-step derivation of the circuit laws. Blind certificate scoring and the common two-arm administration test the research aid within the stated information structure.

## Scored formal overlay, before inference

The formal packet author replaced one formerly constant observable in each of the four affected families while preserving the public question, enumerated objects, truth predicate, initial evidence, selected actuals and all development inputs. An intermediate replacement for the graph problem allowed a different compatible object on the open twin to reveal the answer by luck. The independent every-actual check detected this. The final graph comparator tests whether vertex 2 has degree greater than 2 and preserves ambiguity throughout the open twin.

Reapplying the unchanged fixed policies to the final prospective panel gives **the same aggregate policy counts in each domain**. On either domain the three fixed positions solve 9, 10 and 9 of 12 cases and 3, 4 and 3 of 6 complete families, respectively. Most-distinct and most-balanced each solve 9/12 cases and 3/6 families, including just one of the four cases requiring a measurement. On each formal one-lookup record two observations now vary across allowable states; in the circuits two or three vary. Only one guarantees a decision for every possible reply. The every-actual test confirms all open records remain open under any single item, including after changing the unknown actual within the initial evidence. The full mathematical lookup rule still solves every case, including every family.

These construction checks were completed before admitting the revised holdout. The six formal and six circuit question families remain a small designed sample with a shared observation structure.
