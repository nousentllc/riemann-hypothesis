# Endpoint interpolation protocol — 23 November 2026

Written before running the new bridge controls. An earlier experiment identified 487 as a peak on [256,4096]. It informed the selection of the three local controls. Their scientific status is a test of sign and curvature, not a blind prediction of a peak.

1. Start with the signed ramp sum for the fixed real character modulo four and define a chord through its values at a and b. For every integer in (a,b), compute the contribution of its signed slope jump to the difference from the chord. Verify this identity independently against a sieve-built ramp evaluated at every knot, the interval endpoints and every intervening midpoint. The unknown linear normalization cancels. Perform checks at [486,488], [508,510], [488,490], four adjacent dyadic factor-two bands covering [256,4096], and concentric [487-r,487+r] for r=2,4,8,16,32. The outer bands and concentric cases can have many signed jumps.
2. Using certified rational enclosures, compute the unweighted integral of the positive departure above the chord by piecewise-affine clipping. Compare with the bound from all downward jumps, weighted by half the product of their distances to the endpoints. Expect equality on a single negative jump, zero positive departure for a lone positive jump, and zero for a gap without effective jumps. For negative-jump cases report the continuous weight sandwich b^(-3/2)D to a^(-3/2)D as well as a tighter direct weighted integration where feasible. For mixed cases give the actual slack without a preselected minimum improvement.
3. Derive a conditional bound across holes where both endpoint estimates for the same sum are known. Test the Green triangle's area, sign and 1/8 max over rational integer widths and possible jump locations; show equality at the midpoint. Establish a cubic continuous term and a quadratic discrete correction after changing to logarithmic widths. Report assumptions and isolate the part which cannot be inferred from the source's exceptional-set membership. This is a written derivation, not a claim of a kernel certificate for the interpolation theorem.
