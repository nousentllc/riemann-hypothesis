# Finite comparison selected from the literature sweep

Use six scalar squares and four squares as the adjacent-rank control over the ramified ring of integers Z₂[√2]. Record total, primitive and all-divisible-by-√2 counts separately. Compare all residues through uniformizer depth 10. Also compare targets 0, 1, √2, 1+√2, 2, 4 and 16. Normalize by 2^((n−1)r) at depth r.

Hanke §3 predicts lifting of the good part from 2 ord(2)+1 = 5 for this unit diagonal form. All primitive vectors of this form have a unit coordinate, so the predicted primitive normalized counts at each residue modulo π^5 should agree at all extensions tested. A solution all of whose coordinates lie in πO descends two target levels with normalized multiplier 2^(2−n). A sum of squares has even coefficient of √2 in the integral basis; targets √2 and 1+√2 should already vanish modulo π². All three predictions can fail independently in the code. Verify by direct tuple enumeration at depths 1 and 2, aggregation into the preceding level, source-volume totals, and coordinate halving. Compare the independent four-square unit and valuation-two controls to Thompson §3.1 once normalization is matched. The finite run cannot on its own establish an infinite-depth density or a Lean measure identity.
