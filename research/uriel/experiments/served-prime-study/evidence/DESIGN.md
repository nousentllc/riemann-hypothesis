# Protocol for the served-prime study

Original proof version 40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005. The preceding orbit-loss study identified the sum of log p over served inert primes p0 ≤ p < X′. This pass asks whether that sum is negligible, has a positive limiting proportion, or remains undetermined for the selected family. The following finite checks were specified after preliminary mathematical analysis and before executing the experiments.

1. Hold q fixed and construct two prime discriminants with the original congruence classes, both splitting the odd primes up to 5 and satisfying the compatibility symbol at q. On the eight-prime interval [7,32), require every prime to split for one discriminant and to be in the joint negative-sign class for the other. Search for the least compatible primes above 32 by deterministic trial. Failure to find either case within the declared search bound is reported. These local criteria are a subset of the selection hypotheses.
2. For both sign pairs and cutoffs 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 and 1,000,000, measure the strict prime log-weight and the corresponding positive von Mangoldt sum including prime powers. Check the sign via quadratic reciprocity by a second implementation, the exact four-term character identity, and the boundary and ramification corrections. Record integer counts and prime-set fingerprints. These tests do not simulate the family construction.
3. Test the two-sided secant inequality for a positive measure at each cutoff using the scale fractions 1/4, 1/16 and 1/64. Use exact rational tests on small supports, including an atom exactly at the cutoff, both one-sided boundaries, arbitrary added linear terms and a signed-measure negative control. A validity test is containment. Shrinking brackets and closeness to 1/4 are descriptive observations.
4. Check the proof separately. Identify which hypotheses are uniform in the selected scale and host, what is finite algebra, what follows from source results, and which supplemental declarations are actually kernel-replayed. Test whether the original error allowance for the whole high-prime bucket can be transferred after replacing its uniform prime sum by its true support. A positive lower bound on the component of an allowance never supplies a lower bound on the actual demand-minus-gain gap.

Cutoffs denoted H in the experiments are independent inputs. In the selected-family proof H=X′. No assertion about the scale threshold, failure rate or effective computability of a selected witness is based on the table.
