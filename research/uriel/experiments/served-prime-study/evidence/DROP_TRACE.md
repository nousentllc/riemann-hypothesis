# What do the arithmetic drop rules remove?

The family implements two filters. The arithmetic host supplies its inert primes. The later loss construction decides which of those primes to serve. On the actual upper prime range, the second filter changes the normalized sum of prime logarithms by a quantity tending to zero. This holds for each realization satisfying the same selected-weight bound used by the loss estimate, uniformly in the witness at sufficiently late family scales.

## The prime set and the quantifiers

Write $Y=X'=\exp(x^{\varepsilon/3})$ and let $I$ be the finite set of primes in the host's literal class $\mathrm{inertClass}$ satisfying $p_0\le p<Y$. Here $p_0$ may be taken as the integer ceiling of the source's real cutoff. Let $S$ be the subset with $p\notin L.\mathrm{drop}$, and $D=I\setminus S$. For any finite set $A$ of primes, write $\Theta_A=\sum_{p\in A}\log p$ and $H_A=\sum_{p\in A}\log(p)/p$.

The ambient finite enumeration includes all of $I$. Its endpoint is at least $\lfloor X''\rfloor+1$, and $X'\le X''$. The broader class used to build the ambient set admits signal and inert primes; the prime term in the present trace uses only the inert branch. Every prime in $I$ lies below the high-prime cutoff. The actual refill defines $L.\mathrm{drop}$ by `NoGain` together with the exclusion of all type primes. The construction returns the refill and its instance simultaneously. Type primes cannot be in the ambient signal/inert set, hence on this set $p\in L.\mathrm{drop}$ is equivalent to `NoGain`. The rule `clAt` first tests complement of the drop set, then high, then candidate with signal above threshold, and otherwise marks the prime first-kind. Thus $D=D_1\sqcup D_2$, where $D_j$ is the part of $D$ classified as first or second. This partition keeps the witness, its weights and its prime classifications fixed. ## Bounds recoverable directly from the two source budgets

Let $g=100Kx^{4\varepsilon-1}$, with $K$ fixed for the late family. The first-kind proof bounds $4mTaH_{\mathrm{first}}$ by the sum of the order and band budgets, with the common unit $U=mTb$. Each of the two right-hand budgets is at most $gU$. Set the two test multiplicities $m=T=1$ in this numerical lemma, and use $a=xb$ with $b>0$. Then $H_{D_1}\le H_{\mathrm{first}}\le g/(2x)$. This division uses the auxiliary unit test, so it requires no positive-rank assumption on an arithmetic witness. The source obtains a uniform order constant $C_1$ and selects $C_{\rm ord}=\max(C_1,0)$ before applying these bounds. [First-kind comparison](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/ChainSlopeWindows/OneLeThresholdMulPowers.lean#L1036-L1049) · [Two size estimates](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/ChainSlopeWindows/OneLeThresholdMulPowers.lean#L601-L676) · [Numerical budgets and windows](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/WindowSettingSatisfiable.lean#L147-L164)

For the second-kind sum the chosen weight guarantee reads
$$
 \sum_{p\in{\rm cand}}\frac{\log p}{p}
  \left(2{\bf1}_{\Sigma_p>\theta}+4\Sigma_p\right)\le x^{-9},
 \qquad \Sigma_p\ge0.
$$
On a second-kind prime the coefficient is at least $2+4\theta$. Consequently $H_{D_2}\le x^{-9}/(2+4\theta)$. Retaining the signal improves the coefficient obtained by using the indicator alone. On $D$ one has $\log(p)/Y\le\log(p)/p$. It follows pointwise, for each matching witness, that
$$
 0\le\frac{\Theta_I-\Theta_S}{Y}
 \le\frac{g}{2x}+\frac{x^{-9}}{2+4x^{-\varepsilon}}.
$$
The companion Lean module derives this final upper bound under the actual source window, first-kind and signal hypotheses with the supplied prime subset covered by the two classes. The implication from the actual family classification to that two-class cover is the source trace above. [Joint hypotheses and weighted signal sum](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/PrimeClassLossHypothesesSatisfiable.lean#L36-L55) · [Small-share hypothesis on the same witness](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GlobalTheory/LossRequirementsOfSmallShares.lean#L41-L82) · [Lean source](drop_lean/DroppedPrimeCosts.lean)

## Sharper finite bound for the normalized log sum

The original first-kind budget controls the reciprocal weight at every scale up to a large endpoint. Our target is the ordinary log sum below $Y$. Estimating it directly avoids charging small primes as if they lay near $Y$. The following finite estimate is a mathematical deduction from the pinned congruence and prime-sum results. It was checked separately by finite controls.

Let $r=P.e_2$, $J=\lfloor x^{\varepsilon/4}\rfloor\ge1$, $j=\min(r,\lfloor\log_2 J\rfloor)$, and $M=2^{r+2-j}$. The order alternative places each prime in one of two residue classes, $+1$ or $-1$ modulo $M$. Below $Y$ each class contains at most $Y/M+1$ positive integers. Since $2^j\le J$, one also has $2/M\le J/(2\cdot2^r)$. For the power-band alternative split at $i=1$. At degree one the counted primes lie in the real interval $[(1-\rho)Y,Y)$, containing at most $\rho Y+1$ integers. At every higher degree, $p^2\le(1+\rho)Y$. The finite Chebyshev upper bound in the source gives log-weight at most $\log(4)\sqrt{(1+\rho)Y}$ for their union. Overlapping alternatives may be counted twice in this upper bound. [Signed-order residue implication](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/RankGapBounds/RankGapProofs/PmModulusFacts.lean#L116-L134) · [Dyadic modulus](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/RankGapBounds/RankGapProofs/PmModulusFacts.lean#L231-L247) · [Original FirstHyp](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/WindowSettingSatisfiable.lean#L297-L301) · [Finite prime log-sum upper bound](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/ChainSlopeWindows/AdmissibleSchurLemma.lean#L155-L161)

The resulting inequality is
$$
 \frac{\Theta_{D_1}}{Y}\le
 \left(\frac{2}{M}+\rho+\frac{3}{Y}\right)\log Y
 +\log(4)\sqrt{\frac{1+\rho}{Y}}.
$$
It can be intersected with the earlier $g/(2x)$ bound by taking their minimum. Since $2^r\ge x^2$, $J\le x^{\varepsilon/4}$, $\rho\le x^{-2}$ and $\log Y=x^{\varepsilon/3}$, it is $O(x^{-2+7\varepsilon/12})$ at fixed $\varepsilon>0$. The entire dropped upper inert log sum, after division by $Y$, has that order under the selected-weight cap. For all inert primes below $Y$, the part below $p_0$ adds at most $\log(4)p_0/Y$, exponentially small relative to any power since $p_0\le x^e$ at fixed $e$. The case of exact served fractions from the prior study costs an additional $B/N\le x^{-2+\varepsilon}$. Combining all three gives an $O(x^{-2+\varepsilon})$ difference between the effective mass and total inert prime mass below $Y$, along the same matching family scales. It supplies no numerical value for the total host inert mass. ## Keeping the prime sieve and the actual rank window

A further deduction uses the pinned archive's Brun–Titchmarsh upper bound. The theorem statement gives a universal positive constant. Its displayed proof chooses $48$. For any coprime residue class modulo $q$ and interval $(t,t+y]$ with $t\ge0$ and $y\ge2q$, the number of its primes is at most $48y/(\varphi(q)\log(y/q))$. The half-open endpoints here are the source's endpoints. [Uniform sieve estimate and exhibited constant](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/BrunTitchmarshExistsConst.lean#L49-L55) · [Finite prime interval](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GlobalTwisting/ZerosDisc.lean#L20-L24)

For the two signed residues choose $q=M,\ t=0,\ y=Y$. Both residues are coprime to $M$. Their normalized log-weight below $Y$ is at most
$$
 \frac{96\log Y}{\varphi(M)\log(Y/M)}.
$$
Here $\varphi(M)=M/2$. The window gives $M\le4\cdot2^r\le4x^e$. At fixed $e,\varepsilon$, eventually $Y\ge2M$ and $\log Y/\log(Y/M)\to1$ uniformly. For the degree-one band choose any envelope $R_x\ge\rho$ with $R_x\to0$ and put $y=Y R_x+1,\ t=Y-y,\ q=1$. The added unit covers a prime at the closed lower endpoint of the band. Once $y\ge2$ and $y\le Y$, the corresponding normalized bound is $48y\log Y/(Y\log y)$. Together with the unchanged higher-power term, this gives the finite bound
$$
 \frac{\Theta_{D_1}}{Y}\le
 \frac{96\log Y}{\varphi(M)\log(Y/M)}
 +\frac{48y\log Y}{Y\log y}+\log(4)\sqrt{\frac{1+\rho}{Y}} .
$$
At the generic window alone, $R_x=x^{-2}$ is admissible and $1/M\le J/(4x^2)$, yielding $O(x^{-2+\varepsilon/4})$. The inequalities are applicable to sparse as well as populated prime supports. Their constants provide no prime-density lower bound.

At an actual family point supplied to the loss proof, the data used to establish the generic window carry more information. Write $\tau=\varepsilon/100$. The original size assertions give
$$
 x^{2+8\varepsilon-\tau}\le n\le x^{2+8\varepsilon+\tau},\quad
 k\ge x^{9/2+10\varepsilon-\tau},\quad
 \Lambda\le x^{\varepsilon+\tau}.
$$
Also $n\le o\,2^r$ and $o\le x^\tau$. Hence $2^r\ge x^{2+7.98\varepsilon}$, giving $1/M\le x^{-2-7.73\varepsilon}/4$. The decimal exponents here are exact rationals. [Rank and size ranges](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/ElevenLe.lean#L11-L33) · [Lambda bound](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/ElevenLe.lean#L65-L72) · [Frame order and odd-factor bound](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GlobalTheory/PowFourLtCeilThresholdPrime.lean#L1242-L1265)

To bound $\rho$ use the same family instance's $\Lambda$ and $N_\star=2n x^{-\varepsilon/8}$. With fixed family constants $K_\rho,K_N$, the source sets
$$
 \rho=\frac{4N_\star x^\varepsilon\Lambda}{k}
        +\frac{K_\rho K_N\log(x)\Lambda}{k}
 \ \le\ 8x^{-5/2-19\varepsilon/200}
     +K_\rho K_N\log(x)\,x^{-9/2-449\varepsilon/50}.
$$
Thus one can take $R_x=A x^{-5/2}$ for some fixed positive $A$ beyond a family threshold. The sieve-scaled degree-one band contributes $O(x^{-5/2})$. The contributions from higher powers and second-kind primes are smaller. Since $0<\varepsilon<1/52$ gives $2+7.73\varepsilon<5/2$, the entire normalized drop at these family points obeys
$$
 \frac{\Theta_I-\Theta_S}{Y}=O\!\left(x^{-2-773\varepsilon/100}\right).
$$
This is an upper order for the removed mass. The proof does not exhibit a family attaining it.
