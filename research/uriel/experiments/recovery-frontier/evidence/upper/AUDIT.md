# A global upper bound and the high-dimensional sharpness argument

Fix the principal conductor-one family and the small parameter ε. The underlying group is cyclic of order n a power of two, with n comparable to x^(2+8ε). Write δ=x^(−ε/8), u=ceil(1/δ). All other source constants, including the positive bad-mass constant, stay fixed. The argument quantifies over sets passing the original selection score. It does not identify the pre-existing noncomputable witness. ## What the universal upper bound measures

The previous same-witness transport gives Δ/U = K−R+o(1) and liminf Δ/U≥0. The dyadic occupancy argument gives a positive lower bound for K, uniformly along each compatible family. Therefore limsup R/K≤1. This bound concerns limiting normalized quantities. The small terms in the transport can matter for a finite sample. ## How to approach the bound while preserving the original score

The central issue is to have rare adjacent differences at most prime steps while preserving the population seen by the source's marked tiles, local windows, and cardinality score. The proof uses a fixed auxiliary dimension before taking x sufficiently large. No uniform practical threshold in the dimension is known here.

**Compact support with uniformly small low Fourier coefficients.** Take integer r>32/ε and dimension d>r, with d to be fixed sufficiently large. Normalize Haar measure on the torus (R/Z)^d to total mass one. In one dimension let b be the (d+2)-fold convolution of the uniform probability density on a centered interval of length w/(d+2), w=1/8. Its support has length w. For every integer j≠0, the elementary interval integral gives |b̂(j)|≤min(1, B/|j|)^(d+2), where B=16(d+2). Thus the one-dimensional ℓ¹ norm is at most 4B. Put K₀=B⁴. For the tensor product density b⊗d, the sum of the absolute Fourier coefficients outside the coordinate cube [−K₀,K₀]^d is at most

    d (4B)^(d−1) · 2 B^(d+2) K₀^(−d−1)/(d+1) < 0.001.

Form the arithmetic mean f of M translates of this density. The same tail bound applies to f. Each nontrivial low Fourier coefficient is the original one, of modulus ≤1, times the average of M phases with mean zero when the translates are independent uniform torus vectors. Applying the elementary bounded-variable exponential inequality to real and imaginary parts gives P(failure for one mode)≤4 exp(−M τ²/4) for target τ=1/[100(2K₀+1)^r]. A union bound over (2K₀+1)^d frequencies produces a choice meeting every low coefficient bound. Sufficient M grows polynomially in d for fixed r, since K₀ is polynomial in d. The support lies in a union of M boxes of side 1/8. Its torus difference set D lies in a union of M² boxes of side 1/4, of total volume at most η=M²4^(−d). This tends to zero as d grows with r fixed. After constructing f, fix its translates for the remainder of the argument. The density may have an enormous pointwise maximum but it is finite and independent of x.

**Embed the actual cyclic group.** Choose coordinates on Z/n and independent uniform α₁,…,α_d in Z/n. Map t to tα/n on the torus. For a source-tested step h of order L≥x^(ε/4), the vector 2hα/n is uniform on the grid of order m=L/2 in every coordinate. In particular m≥u²/3 eventually. Call a nonzero low frequency k slow when ||2k·hα/n||≤u^(−1/2), where ||·|| is circle distance to zero. The probability of r rationally independent slow frequencies is O_d(u^(−r/2)). To see this, choose the integer matrix formed by these rows and a nonsingular r-column minor of determinant t. Conditional on the remaining α-coordinates, a congruence for the selected output vector has at most |t|^r input solutions on the m-grid. Indeed multiplication by the adjugate confines each coordinate of a kernel vector to the kernel of multiplication by t modulo m, of size at most |t|. The target cube has at most (2m/√u+1)^r possible output vectors. Summing over the finitely many low-frequency choices changes only the fixed constant. This holds for nonunit determinants and needs independence of rows over the rationals. Because u^(−r/2)=O(x^(−εr/16))=o(x^−2), even the original bad-orbit budget can absorb resonant steps.

If the slow frequencies have rank <r, their intersection with the frequency cube has at most (2K₀+1)^(r−1) elements. An injective coordinate projection onto rank-many coordinates proves this. The nonzero slow contribution is below 1/100 by the chosen τ. In either parity subsequence of a length-u cell, a fast mode has average in modulus O(u^(−1/2)), by the geometric-series bound and the doubled phase. Summing its contribution costs at most O_d(u^(−1/2)). The Fourier tail is <0.001. Thus f's average in every parity of every such cell is within 0.02 of one eventually on these steps, uniformly over initial positions. No assertion of independent neighboring sites of the support is needed. The same estimates apply to cell lengths differing by O(1).

With probability 1−o(1) no nonzero low k is exactly annihilated by 2α/n. For each fixed k this follows by conditioning on a nonzero coordinate and counting its grid kernel. On that event the whole-host mean z of f on the grid and the means on each root parity half are within 0.001 of one by the Fourier tail. Normalize rates q_t=δ f(tα/n)/z so that their sum is exactly δn. These rates are in [0,C_dδ] and the original source law remains the uniform independent Bernoulli(δ) law when judging S(T).

For the actual source prime weights ω_p=log(p)/p, at fixed host the expected weighted fraction of resonant steps is o(x^−2) by the preceding uniform probability bound. Markov's inequality lets us take that fraction o(x^−2) with probability 1−o(1), choosing a deterministic tending-to-zero slack after multiplying by x². If the weight sum is zero there is no constraint. For each possible step of order L, the image hα/n is uniform on the product L-grid. Its probability of hitting the difference boxes is at most M²(1/4+2/L)^d. All directions of order at most √n account for O(n^−1/2) of this dyadic group. Thus the expected fraction of directions landing in D is at most η+o(1). A fraction at most 2η+o(1) is attainable simultaneously with the two high-probability embedding requirements. Fix such an embedding. Each construction uses the given arithmetic host and its prime steps, with a single embedding across all of them.

**Pass the actual marked, window and inner score tests.** Draw T independently at the rates q_t. On steps whose image is outside D, the geometric support admits no two adjacent retained points in either orientation, for any draw. On nonresonant source-tested steps every length-u cell has total inclusion mass 1±0.05 eventually and even-site mass between 0.45 and 0.55. Both an empty cell and a singleton at an even site therefore have probability >0.12 eventually, uniformly: use the product of (1−q_t) and, for the singleton, multiply by the sum over eligible even t of q_t/(1−q_t). The maximum q_t tends to zero. These estimates concern the original mark predicate, including its parity restriction.

Condition on a tagged point being retained. Omit the at most one trial tile containing it. Among the remaining M₀ distinct disjoint tiles in each required block, a tile of 6r_G+1 cells has mark probability at least 0.12^(6r_G+1). The source takes M₀≥(2+8ε+3) log(x)10^(6r_G+1). A union bound over starts therefore gives o(x^−2) failure. Here r_G is the original tile parameter, distinct from r above. The short-window test has O(δ^K+L δ^(K+1)) failure for its original K≥8(2+8ε+2)/ε and bounded L₂. The anchor occurs in at most L₂ starts. At other starts K+1 successes are required. Constants depending on C_d and on K are fixed before x tends to infinity. For the long-window test, G′ is an integer multiple of u. Splitting a window into cells bounds its total marginal mass by 1.1 G′δ eventually. For k=N₁−1≥6e G′δ and k≥(2+8ε+3)log₂ x, conditioning on the anchor leaves the probability of k other successes at most (1.1eG′δ/k)^k. This follows by summing products of marginal probabilities over k-element subsets. Union over starts still costs o(x^−2). The resonant directions can be charged their entire expected top count δn times their prime weights. Consequently E B(T)=o(C_b x^−2 δn Σω_p) on the original source prime set. No increase in C_b is needed. [Three original tests](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SubspaceCounting/GoodOrbitIntervalTileCondition.lean#L9-L26) · [Thresholds](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/UnramifiedQuadraticSetting.lean#L235-L252)

For the score's count term, Var(|T|)=(1+o(1))δn, since max q_t=o(1). Bounded independent Bernoulli summands satisfy the triangular-array Lindeberg criterion when the total variance diverges. Thus P(||T|−δn|≤½√(δn)) tends to the strictly positive standard-normal probability of [−1/2,1/2]. The first score term on this event is at most 1/8. The bad-mass score tends to zero in probability by the preceding expectation bound. The two root parity counts concentrate around their means by variance O(δn). Hence one positive original-weight mask satisfies all these conclusions. Its score beats (1−δ)/2, a lower bound on the original uniform-product mean. The source's internal comparison and exported tests both hold. If desired, standard-normal convergence here follows directly by expanding characteristic functions of the independent centered bounded summands: total normalized third absolute moment is O((δn)^−1/2), so the characteristic function tends to exp(−t²/2) for each fixed t.

**Transport the geometry into recovered gain.** On a blocked direction, every first successor of a retained source is a hole. Throughout the relevant prime interior the source-connected calculation gives recovery at least λ times the orbit's odd retained count, up to o(N_v λ). The incumbent local allowance has the same leading odd-count average over directions by the earlier study. Summed missed and dropped retained mass costs o(1) after normalization, uniformly for a compatible family. For an upper bound on the allowance lost to the directions in D it suffices to charge N tops on every such direction. The prior uniform moving-host prime-step estimate gives K−R≤(1/2)·#eligible-directions/n+o(1)≤η+o(1), where “eligible” in this sentence means an image in the *difference boxes*. Root odd mass alone gives K≥0.12 eventually up to an o(1) adjustment, independently of d, since half the directions generate the whole group and root odd mass exceeds 0.48N eventually. Thus the limiting recovered fraction is at least 1−η/0.12. Let d grow only after the scale limit. Arbitrarily small η gives supremum one. A diagonal choice over increasingly late hosts can also make the ratio tend to one. This does not compute a fraction for the originally selected hidden mask. ## Finite design class with a different ceiling

Suppose a fixed dyadic quotient has a nonnegative rate on each leaf, in units of δ, with mean one and maximum c. Suppose the selected family realizes the prescribed masses and on the significant prime range has enough first-step edges whenever the leaf geometry allows either orientation, as in the matched-profile constructions. For each fixed dyadic coset having positive odd mass, the source mark condition requires positive even support. Otherwise all retained tops on that coset would be bad for every step generating it, an inadmissible positive mass at a fixed depth. For any node v of depth j, let l_v,o_v be the fractions of *all* retained tops in its left (even) and right (odd) children in the profile limit. A child containing mass l_v occupies at least l_v·2^(j+1)/c of its available residues. By translating the larger child support by a point of the other child, at least max(l_v,o_v)·2^(j+1)/c of the generator directions allow an adjacent pair when o_v>0. The contribution of this node to total unnormalized odd exposure S is w_j o_v, with w_j=2^(−j−1). Therefore at least c^−1 Σ_v o_v max(l_v,o_v) survives. Continue the dyadic refinement inside each homogeneous leaf so the tail carries its actual exposure.

The sum Σ l_v o_v=1/2 telescopes from parent mass squared minus child masses squared, since the terminal measure has no atoms. The sum Σ w_j² over all nodes is 1/2. Expanding Σ(o_v−w_j)²≥0 yields Σ o_v²≥2S−1/2. Consequently Σ o_v max(l_v,o_v)≥max(1/2,2S−1/2)≥S. The recovered fraction in this design class is at most 1−1/c. This is a restricted rate and mixing result. Every particular fixed-coset rate of a score-compatible homogeneous profile is at most 6e by the earlier occupancy argument, giving the looser 1−1/(6e) for these profiles. For a bounded-rate fixed quotient with no guarantee of realized edges, mere positivity of rates does not imply the same upper bound. The torus construction is highly nonuniform *inside* each fixed dyadic leaf. Its pointwise cap C_d can also grow without bound across increasingly sharp constructions. ## Reproduction and limitations

The two designs predate their corresponding finite runs. The 60 exact two-dimensional grid checks include nonsingular integer matrices with nonunit determinants. All 12 repeated-row negative controls defeated a naive rank-two grid bound. All 504 parity-cell geometric sums passed at absolute floating-point tolerance 2×10⁻¹⁴. The dimension certificate uses the bit length k of 2K₀+1 and M=2^s with s=3+2(7+rk)+bitlength(dk+4). This conservative M makes the frequency union bound strictly smaller than one. For ε=1/64, r=2049, the logarithm (base two) of M²4^(−d) is 541080 at d=65536 and −1293856 at d=1048576. The smaller dimension fails this certificate; the larger proves a volume estimate, without generating that many translates. The periodic tree inequality passed 168 compatible binary mask/ordering tests, with 852 excluded for empty canonical even children, and 384 nonconstant fully supported cases. The 65,260 finite overlap tests also satisfied |D∪(−1−D)|≥min(m,2min(e,o)) for the tested sizes. This additional observation is not used in either upper bound.
