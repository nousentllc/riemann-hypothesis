# Transferring a high-dimensional profile through the original score

This note verifies the selector implications used by the manuscript's near-complete recovery construction. It starts with two quantitative hypotheses on a single embedding and profile, and uses the pinned window definitions to reach exactly the original selector's test. The two hypotheses require the separate torus and resonance argument.

* The trial probabilities on the actual host satisfy \(\sum_t p_t=\delta n\) and \(0\le p_t\le C_d\delta\). The dimension and \(C_d\) are fixed before taking \(x\to\infty\).
* Outside steps carrying \(o(x^{-2})\) of the total **selection** weight \(\sum_p(\log p)/p\), every aligned \(u=\lceil 1/\delta\rceil\) cell on an eligible orbit has both parity sums in \([0.46,0.54]\) eventually. These bounds refer to the normalized probabilities and are uniform in the cell and orbit start. Selection is assessed at all eligible primes and uses an entirely different weight from the ultimate prime log-mass of missing pairs.

Draw the mask with independent Bernoulli coordinates at these trial rates. For an ordinary step, the cell's total mean is between 0.92 and 1.08 and its canonical-even mean is at least 0.46. Since \(\max p_t\to0\), the probability all are empty is at least \(e^{-1.09}\) eventually (for example apply \(\log(1-p)\ge-p/(1-\max p_t)\)). The probability of exactly one even site is the all-empty probability times \(\sum_{t\text{ even}}p_t/(1-p_t)\), at least \(0.46e^{-1.09}>0.15\) eventually. Thus every individual empty or even-singleton requirement has probability exceeding 0.12. Disjoint tiles use independent sites. After conditioning on a particular positive-rate site being retained, discard the trial tile containing it. Union over starts and the source \(M_0\) give \(o(x^{-2})\) for the marked-tile failure. A zero-rate tag has zero contribution to bad mass.

For the long-window failure put \(k=N_1-1\). The filtered long windows in the source have length \(G'\), an integer multiple of the cell width. Full uniform start control implies the mean is at most \(1.2G'\delta\) eventually. Removing the anchor cannot raise the mean of the other trials. For independent, possibly unequal Bernoulli coordinates, the probability of at least \(k\) successes is bounded by the sum over successful \(k\)-subsets, at most \((\sum p_t)^k/k!\le(e\sum p_t/k)^k\). Since \(k\ge6eG'\delta\), this is at most \(0.2^k\). With \(k\ge(h+3)\log_2x\), union over \(L=O(x^h)\) starts is \(o(x^{-2})\). In a short window of bounded length \(L_2\), at most \(L_2\) starts contain the tag and require \(K\) further successes. The other starts require \(K+1\). Thus the union costs \(O((C_d\delta)^K+L(C_d\delta)^{K+1})=o(x^{-2})\) even at equality of the ceiling exponent, since \(\delta^K\le x^{-h-2}\).

Every exceptional prime costs at most \(\sum_t p_t=\delta n\) in expected bad mass, so the second hypothesis and the ordinary-step estimates imply \(\mathbb EB=o(M)\) for the *original* positive constant \(C_b\). When the total eligible source weight vanishes, \(B=0\) identically. No hypothesis on how the different prime events depend on one another is needed. The empirical count does not have to equal its mean exactly to pass the private test. Set \(V=\sum_t p_t(1-p_t)\sim\delta n\), since \(C_d\delta\to0\). The centered independent coordinates have absolute third moments summing to at most \(\sum_t p_t(1-p_t)=V\); hence the standardized count converges to a standard normal by the Lyapunov CLT. In particular \(|N-\delta n|\le\frac12\sqrt{\delta n}\) has probability tending to a strictly positive constant. This bounds the original count-score term by \(1/8\). Intersecting with \(B/M=o(1)\) and any other events holding with probability tending to one yields a single mask with \(S\le1/8+o(1)<\mathbb E_\delta S\), where the original *uniform* score mean is at least \((1-\delta)/2\). Every fixed finite mask has positive probability in that uniform law. Conductor-one shares are automatic. This deduction transfers the proposed profile to the original selection rule without replacing that rule or assigning probabilities to the incumbent's hidden outcome. ## Exact count and unbiased exposure by quantization

For each fixed dimension, the manuscript's stronger variant removes the need for count fluctuations. Let \(f\) be its fixed bounded continuous nonnegative torus profile of Haar mean one. Pick \(0<t<1/128\) such that no positive multiple of \(t\) is a positive atom of the distribution of \(f\) or its maximum. Only countably many mesh sizes are prohibited. Set \(g=t\lfloor f/t\rfloor\). Each positive bin has positive Haar measure if its level is attained, by continuity and the intermediate value property. Its boundary is Haar-null. The zero bin \(\{f<t\}\) can have a large atom at zero but its boundary is contained in \(\{f=t\}\). The support of positive bins is contained in the original support. Quantization therefore adds no pairs on blocked directions.

For random host embeddings, each fixed-index dyadic coset becomes Haar distributed in probability as the host order grows. For a fixed nonzero Fourier character the average over its coset is zero unless its frequency annihilates the underlying cyclic subgroup, which has probability tending to zero as the subgroup order tends to infinity. Approximation of continuous functions and then of fixed Haar-continuity sets gives the bin counts. This reasoning applies to finitely many bins or cosets at a time. In particular all used stratum populations are at least \(c_d n\) for some fixed \(c_d>0\) eventually on a high-probability event. The empirical mean \(\bar g\) is between \(1-t-o(1)\) and \(1+o(1)\).

Within each positive bin of host size \(m_i\) and level \(g_i\), sample a uniform subset whose size rounds \(\delta m_i g_i/\bar g\). Adjust the bounded total rounding error in a positive bin to make \(N\) the integer nearest \(\delta n\). Each sampling rate, including after conditioning on one retained tag, differs from \(\delta g_i/\bar g\) by \(O_d(1/n)\). The cell means change by a bounded amount governed by \(t\), since \(0\le f-g<t\). The underlying smoothed Fourier estimates should be applied to \(f\), retaining this explicit slack, rather than approximating a discontinuous quantization uniformly by a polynomial.

To apply the preceding source checks, compare each local event to independent Bernoulli trials with the conditional stratum rates. On \(\ell_i\) inspected sites in a stratum of remaining size \(m_i'\), its probability for any binary pattern is at most \(m_i'^{\ell_i}/(m_i')_{\ell_i}\) times the product probability. The bound follows by taking the numerator of the falling-factorial pattern law term by term. All tested blocks have total length \(O(\delta^{-1}\log x)\). Thus the product of these multipliers over the fixed strata is \(1+o(1)\), since \(\sum_i\ell_i^2/m_i'=o(1)\). Degenerate zero-rate bins can be ignored. This preserves the bounds even for rare conditional failures. Consequently the original score tends to zero in probability for these exact counts.

Finally, on every fixed depth of the dyadic tree each bin has matching limiting fractions in each of the finitely many cosets by the same equidistribution. Sampling without replacement has variance \(O(N)\) for any bounded linear score. The relative transferred mass of each fixed coset therefore tends to its uniform mass. At each of finitely many levels the selected mass in the source's canonical odd child tends to one half, independent of the host's choice of canonical orientations. The remaining aggregate exposure tail is at most \(2^{-j}\) beyond fixed depth \(j\). Taking host limits before \(j\to\infty\), or imposing finitely many levels at a slowly growing depth by diagonal choice, gives \(\mathcal K_x\to1/4\) on compatible choices. Intersect these probability-tending-to-one properties with the manuscript's admissible embedding and small-difference conditions. Exact cardinality and the original internal score are then enforced on the same mask throughout its arithmetic tests.
