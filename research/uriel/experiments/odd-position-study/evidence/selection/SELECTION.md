# Which retained set can the original selection argument support?

The pinned construction selects one finite set of tops at a host. Its exported conditions control its size, its distribution among distinguished cosets, and the cost of bad orbits. They do not specify an odd fraction. We revisited the selection argument before the witness is chosen. It permits a second property for the very same set and admits different parity biases at conductor one.

## The host, its labels, and the point where the choice occurs

Fix the primitive input character \(\psi\). Its level has \(o=|\langle\psi\rangle|\), the cardinality of the cyclic subgroup of characters generated by \(\psi\). The field construction returns a frame of this index at every scale. Both the rank construction and the final `EAtH` retain the equality. In particular, principal conductor one has \(o=1\), so the distinguished subgroup \(A_0=\langle a_0\rangle\) is all of \(A\). The numeric labels of the field's frame may order the elements differently from their additive cyclic coordinates. [Level definition and index][level] · [Fixed-character field][field] · [Fixed index in the actual host construction][supply1] · [Return of the same index and shares][supply2]

The cyclotomic supply calls `hostOfFieldOf`. With the field and prime-pair row already fixed, it calls `topsChoiceH`, replaces the host's retained set with that particular \(T\), and returns its size, share and bad-orbit certificates. The host computes each \(h_p\) from the field's arithmetic Frobenius and Artin labels. This choice precedes \(T\). The canonical base \(b_h(t)\) is the least numeric label of an orbit element from which a multiple of \(h\) reaches \(t\). An inert prime uses step \(h_p\). Each offset modulo \(\operatorname{ord}(h_p)\) is unique, and changing the step to another generator of the same even cyclic subgroup preserves the parity relative to this base. At two later indices related to the same source host, equality of ranks and injectivity of point labels identify the retained set and every Frobenius label. The formula uses the same canonical bases there. ## What the product measure selects

Write \(n=|A|,\ d=x^{-\varepsilon/8}\). Independently include each \(t\in A\) with probability \(p_t\). The original instantiation uses \(p_t=d\) in \(A_0\) and \(3d/4\) outside. For each coset \(c\), let \(Y_c\) be its sampled count minus its expected count. Write \(B\) for the source's sum of bad retained-top counts weighted by \(\log(p)/p\), and \(m=C_b x^{-2}dn\sum\omega_p\). With \(D^2=2dn\), the proof selects a positive-probability set whose score is at most the mean of
\[
 F(T)=\frac{\sum_cY_c^2}{D^2}+\frac{B(T)}{4m}.
\]
The two expected terms are at most \(1/2\) and \(1/4\). Lean real division assigns zero to \(0/0\). When \(m=0\), the source proves separately from \(\mathbb E B=0\) that every positive-probability selected outcome has \(B=0\). Thus an expectation budget of \(1/4\) is available before the score reaches one. The original proof rounds the second term up to \(1/2\) and exports no parity statistic. [Product weights and density][pw] · [Choice of profile and score inputs][group] · [Full finite selection proof][select]

Let \(P\) be a finite set of primes with even-order steps known before selecting \(T\). For each of their canonical orbits put \(s_p(t)=+1\) at odd offsets and \(-1\) at even offsets. For arbitrary nonnegative weights \(v_p\) fixed at this stage let \(W=\sum v_p>0\), \(D_p(T)=\sum_{t\in T}s_p(t)\), and \(\mu_p=\sum_t p_t s_p(t)\). Then
\[
  \mathbb E\big[(D_p(T)-\mu_p)^2\big]
    =\sum_t p_t(1-p_t)s_p(t)^2\le dn.
\]
Linearity of expectation permits averaging these squared errors over arbitrarily correlated prime directions. Add \(\sum_p v_p(D_p-\mu_p)^2/(4dnW)\) to the score before selecting. Its expected value is at most \(1/4\). The same witness has all the original bounds and obeys
\[
 \sum_{p\in P} v_p(D_p-\mu_p)^2\le4dnW,\qquad
 \sum_{p\in P} v_p|D_p-\mu_p|\le2W\sqrt{dn}.
\]
The last inequality is weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. Its right side still bounds every subsum, even for a subset depending on \(T\) or on later lift data. Use \(v_p=\log p\) on a fixed high inert range, keeping the original bad-orbit cost at its original weights \(\log(p)/p\). A two-weight assertion is essential here. For \(P\) empty the additional inequality is automatic. [Squared-deviation selector](JointParitySelection.lean) · [Application to the original Good-orbit test](GoodSamplingProfile.lean)

The source's actual bad-event estimate holds for **every** pointwise profile \(3d/4\le p_t\le d\). Restrict the profile to \(b+i h_p\). The orbit marginal identity factors out \(p_t\) for the conditioning point, and the original tile, interval, and clustering estimates apply to the other coordinates. The original proof already performs this argument with two unequal probabilities. Our wrapper passes the full range of profiles through those same estimates. Its conclusion includes \(||T|-\sum p_t|\le\sqrt{2dn}\), the original bound on \(B\), and the new squared-deviation bound. At conductor one, \(A_0=A\), so the coset share identity is automatic. Since \(dn\to\infty\), any mean count between \(3dn/4\) and \(dn\) also yields the original size interval \([dn/2,2dn]\) eventually. For general index the tilted profiles below need a separate check of all coset means. [Inhomogeneous conditional tail estimate][notgood] · [Orbit marginal under arbitrary rates][marginal] · [Application of both inputs by the original selector][apply] · [Actual selection contract for the host][topsbase]

An independent simultaneous version costs a logarithm. There are at most \(n\) possible step directions. For each direction the centered summands are independent, their absolute values are at most one and their total variance is at most \(dn\). The exponential-moment bound \(\mathbb{E}e^{uX}\le\exp[u^2\operatorname{Var}(X)/(2(1-u/3))]\) for \(0\le u<3\) follows by expanding the exponential, using \(|X|^k\le X^2\) for \(k\ge2\) and \(k!\ge2\cdot3^{k-2}\). Applying this bound to each sign of the centered sum and multiplying the moments yields the two-sided tail \(2\exp[-z^2/(2(dn+z/3))]\). For \(L=\log(16n)\) and \(z=\sqrt{2dnL}+2L/3\), the probability that any step exceeds \(z\) is at most \(1/8\). The original score exceeds one with probability at most \(3/4\). Thus a positive-probability intersection of both desired events exists. This alternative gives control simultaneously in every even direction at the same retained set. The preceding weighted estimate has the smaller error for a specified weight system. The simultaneous tail argument is a written deduction.

## The original profile and the biased alternatives

On every ambient orbit with even period, the two parity signs cancel. The original profile therefore has mean
\[
 \mu_p=(d/4)\sum_{t\in A_0}s_p(t).
\]
If the image of \(h_p\) in \(A/A_0\) has odd order, any orbit meeting \(A_0\) revisits it at odd strides and contains an even number of visits. Their signs alternate. Otherwise the absolute value is at most \(d|A_0|/4\). The original profile has expected count \(dn(3o+1)/(4o)\). The normalized signed bias on a strengthened selection is thus bounded, in weighted average, by \(1/(3o+1)+O((dn)^{-1/2})\) when the index is fixed. At \(o=1\) every mean is zero. An equally feasible original-profile selection has weighted odd fraction \(1/2+O((dn)^{-1/2})\) before losses. This is an existential assertion at the choice of tops. The original selected representative is given only by existence.

At conductor one let \(A\) be the cyclic dyadic group of the field. Its generators have the entire group as orbit, hence the same least base. Their odd positions form one common half \(U\) of \(A\). Choose \(p_t=d\) in \(U\), \(3d/4\) in its complement. Every generator then has expected odd fraction, meaning the ratio of expected counts, \(4/7\). Every non-generating even-order orbit stays in one half, its inclusion probability is constant, and its expected odd fraction is \(1/2\). For a generator mass fraction \(q\) among the chosen untruncated prime weights, the odd fraction for the additional selected witness is
\[
       \frac12+\frac{q}{14}+O((dn)^{-1/2}).
\]
The opposite tilt changes the sign. The study's separately proved prime-step comparison gives generator share tending to \(1/2\) on the high inert range. This yields **\(15/28\)** and **\(13/28\)** as alternative limiting odd fractions. For the passage to the served set, discarded primes cost at most their full log weight and discarded tops at most their omitted count at each prime. Both normalized losses vanish by the independently established served-prime bounds. A choice of top set may change which later lifts are served. The estimates apply to every lift of that new host carrying the original loss hypotheses. This does not identify the bias of a previously fixed hidden witness, or the value of the local minimum in the discriminant charge. ## How much bias can this family of product measures realize?

Fix a depth \(k\) before taking the scale large. Canonical bases orient each binary subgroup split. At each split both children have equal size, even if the least numeric label is unrelated to ordinary cyclic coordinates. Uniformly sampling a group element gives each of the \(2^k\) parity words probability \(2^{-k}\). At level \(j<k\), the conditional asymptotic prime weight is \(2^{-j-1}\). Choosing rates constant in each depth-\(k\) cell makes the deeper even-period orbits unbiased within the cell. The expected odd fraction for an individual cell, after assigning half of the unresolved tail, has the form \(u_j=(j+1/2)/2^k\), each value occurring once. Here the score counts odd positions, regardless of whether a local inequality later charges for them.

Write \(a_j=p_t/d\in[3/4,1]\) on cell \(j\). The limiting odd fraction at fixed depth is \(R=\sum a_j u_j/\sum a_j\). For each proposed ratio \(R\), increasing \(a_j\) increases the numerator minus \(R\) times the denominator exactly when \(u_j>R\). The maximum therefore uses high rates above its own threshold and low rates below; ties are immaterial. In the continuum limit the root equation is
\[
 \frac34(1/2-R)+\frac18(1-R)^2=0,
 \qquad R=4-2\sqrt3=0.535898384862\ldots.
\]
The smallest fraction in this class of profiles is \(1-R\). Taking the large-scale limit at each fixed depth and then increasing the depth gives fractions arbitrarily close to either endpoint. A family attaining the endpoint itself would use a diagonal choice or uniform shell estimates. The endpoint bounds the ratio of means of these profiles. It is not an extremum for every deterministic witness satisfying the published conditions.

## Checks and reproduction

* All 40,320 label orders of the cyclic group of order eight had the same eight point scores for its seven nonzero directions. A generator always induced the same odd class for that labelling, whereas each nongenerator was balanced separately inside both root halves.
* The 256 subsets for an inhomogeneous profile of the same group had total probability exactly one. With the independent test weights recorded in the output, the exact weighted centered second moment was \(403/8\), below its bound of \(104\). This calculation uses every subset, including the empty one.
* All 65,536 two-rate patterns at depth four were checked. The largest midpoint-weighted ratio was \(943/1760\). At depth twelve the threshold characterization gave \(62290839/116236288\). The computation tests canonical parity and the sampling algebra. It does not generate an asymptotic arithmetic host.
