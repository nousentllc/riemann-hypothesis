# Fixed-witness argument and the coefficient that can be attained

This is a source-derived mathematical deduction. The new Lean modules handle the local two-sided comparison and the finite selection input. They do not state the final family limit. The preceding study [identified the unit offset and its all-inert quarter-weight](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/pull/9). This note records the additional dependencies needed to average its actual charge. In the paper, $N$ denotes the retained count, $n$ the group size, $\delta=x^{-\varepsilon/8}$ and the offset unit is $\lambda=b/H$.

## 1. Characters for every step on the same host

Let $G=\{\pm1\}^2\times A$ and $M=Q_Sqd$. Write $g_p$ for the unramified Artin image. It has the two quadratic signs and additive step $h_p$ as its three coordinates. Host-wide ChosenThin supplies one positive constant for all read characters and all $y\ge H/3$. The original supplies primitive representatives, their agreement on units and the identification of the principal character. One can also obtain the representative by restricting a group character along the Artin map and primitively inducing the resulting Dirichlet character. The Artin map is onto. Fix $g$. Expand its indicator by group-character orthogonality with coefficients $\overline{\chi(g)}/|G|$, of total absolute value one. On unramified powers this recovers a real nonnegative measure with atoms $\log p$ whenever $g_p^j=g$ at $p^j$. To apply the source to the smoothed transform $F_g(y)=\sum a_n(y/n-1)_+$, first use primitive characters on all integers. Their smoothed sums contribute $\mathbf1_{\chi=1}y\log y$, a linear function, and an error of modulus $\le y\exp(-c x^{\varepsilon/8})$. For each $p\mid M$ subtract its prime-power terms. If their coefficients are $b_j$ with $|b_j|\le\log p$, this correction equals $y\sum_{j\ge1}b_j/p^j+O(\log y+\log p)$ for $y\ge2$. Indeed each retained constant term costs $\log p$, there are at most $\log y/\log p$, and the geometric tail after the last retained power costs at most $2\log p$. Thus the possibly large ramified contribution is linear to sufficient accuracy. Its coefficient stays fixed across the three sample points.

After the correction, the coefficient of $y\log y$ is $1/(4n)$. The bracket from the earlier study bounds the normalized strict/inclusive cumulative weights using $H,H(1-r),H(1+r)$. The normalized error is $O(r+\eta_x/r)$, where one may take $\eta_x=e^{-c x^{\varepsilon/8}}+C(1+\log H)\log(2M)/H$. Constants here are uniform in $g$; the total absolute Fourier weight is one. With $r=\sqrt{\eta_x}$ every normalized error is smaller than $x^{-D}$ eventually for any fixed $D$. Discarding powers with exponent at least two costs at most $\sqrt H\log H$. The polynomial lower cutoff and a possible prime exactly at $H$ also have super-polynomially small normalized weight. Consequently the high-band log-weight for each $(-1,-1,h)$ differs from $H/(4n)$ by $O_D(H x^{-D})$. Since $n$ is polynomial in $x$, summing over the whole group still gives super-polynomial decay. No fixed-field asymptotic is substituted for a moving field. ## 2. From a local positive charge to a group average

The [local trace](local/SOURCE.md) keeps the favorable lift correction and subtracts all unused odd partner columns. On the interior band $[\sqrt H,H/(1+x^{-1}+\beta)]$ this sandwiches the positive local difference between the count of unpaired odd tops times $\lambda$ plus or minus $o(N_v\lambda)$ uniformly in prime, orbit and lift. A column at exponent one can remain unused only if its odd target is absent and therefore thin. Higher odd columns have a geometric tail. The strips that were removed cost $o(H)$ in log-weight by an all-integer bound. The [transport trace](transport/TRANSPORT.md) retains the same orbit, same prime, same data and unit coefficient in the demand-minus-gain inequality.

When comparing with all retained points, omitted points and dropped primes cost $o(H)$ in normalized point log-weight by [the checked service and drop comparison](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/1d893ba17074fc74c4546e4a49e38af18f774829/research/uriel/experiments/served-prime-study/evidence/lean/README.md). On a participating orbit all its retained points share service. The raw count of adjacent retained pairs at step $h$ is $|T\cap(T+h)|$. Its sum over $h$ is exactly $N^2$. After uniform prime averaging, divided by inert-prime mass times $N$, it is $N/n+o(1)$. Therefore paired retained odds have vanishing weight. There is no assumption of independence between $T$ and the prime step here. An arbitrary data-dependent test bounded by one on $A$ can use the uniform point estimates. This gives $\mathcal K_x=(2N)^{-1}\sum_{t\in T}w(t)+o(1)$, with $w(t)$ the uniform-step odd exposure. Odd-order elements, if present, inject as a subgroup into $A/A_0$ and contribute at most $o/n$ to a fraction. The symbol $o$ here denotes the subgroup index.

## 3. The same witness has a bounded rank density

The actual fixed-character host preserves $o=[A:A_0]$ for all family indices. Its distinguished cyclic group has order $n_0=n/o=2^s$. Its representatives are least numeric labels of cosets, independent of the generator of a given subgroup. In particular a relabeling can change which child is scored odd but cannot change the uniform grid of conditional scores $w_0$ in any $A_0$ coset. Each initial $k$-bit rank bin corresponds to one coset of $2^k A_0$ in each $A_0$ coset. Take any fixed $k$. A cell has $L=n_0/2^k$ points. All generators of its step subgroup have order $L\gg x^{\varepsilon/4}$ eventually, and their high inert band has log-weight $H/(4o2^{k+1})+o(H)$. The bad-point sum includes these primes independently of service or the near-power exclusion. Suppose a cell contains more than $LN_1/G'$ retained points. In the interval condition a start is allowed at every position; since $G'\le L$ eventually, each point belongs to exactly $G'$ windows. The cell must be bad for every such prime. With $N_1/G'\sim6\mathrm e\delta$ and $N\le2\delta n$, the cell would contain a positive fraction of $N$, bounded below by a number depending only on $o,k$. Multiplying by the normalized log-weight of its generating primes lower-bounds $B/N$ because $1/p>1/H$. But $B/N=o(1)$. Thus every cell eventually meets the cap. The assertion follows uniformly even if the overfull cell changes with $x$. Summing the cell cap over all $o$ cosets and dividing by $N\ge\delta n/2$ bounds the retained mass in any dyadic rank bin by $(12\mathrm e+o(1))2^{-k}$. Spread the mass of each bin uniformly over its interval. This changes the mean by at most $2^{-k}$ and gives a density at most $R=12\mathrm e+o(1)$. For any density bounded by $R\ge1$, the integral of the survival function for $1-w_0$ is at least $\int_0^{1/R}(1-Ru)\,du=1/(2R)$. Both lower and upper mean bounds are sharp for this density relaxation, by filling the bottom or top interval of width $1/R$. Take the large-scale limit and then the depth limit. ## 4. Within a different selection experiment the endpoints are exact

At $o=1$ the profile in the [selection trace](selection/SELECTION.md) may use the finite deterministic score $w(t)$ as its argument before $T$ is chosen. The original bad-probability estimate is uniform for inclusion probabilities anywhere in $[3\delta/4,\delta]$. For independent coordinates, the variance of the sum of any fixed $[0,1]$ score is at most $\delta n$. One subset simultaneously satisfies the size bound, bad-mass bound and concentration of that score. After forming the host with that same subset, use sections 1 and 2. A profile equal to its upper rate above $4-2\sqrt3$ and lower rate elsewhere attains the upper concentrated-mean charge $2-\sqrt3$. Its reflection attains $\sqrt3-3/2$. These values describe possible admissible constructions. They do not identify the particular witness hidden in the original existential proof or give lower bounds for demand minus credited gain.
