# Which odd tops can still consume the leading allowance?

On the original selected family, almost all of the logarithmic prime weight sees a binary rule. An odd retained top whose immediately preceding position is a retained even top uses zero of the leading allowance in the refined upper bound. An odd retained top without that predecessor uses the full allowance. “Almost all” means that the omitted prime weight divided by the upper cutoff tends to zero. This classifies an upper bound. The actual deficit can be smaller.

## Orbit inequality and the source of the allowance

Write $`H=X'`$, $`A=a_{\rm scale}`$, $`B_0=b'=A/H`$, $`\lambda=B_0/x`$, and $`\eta=L.\mathrm{etaSz}`$. At an odd selected position $`y`$ on a served central orbit, write $`B=b_y`$, $`o={\rm off}_{j,y}`$, and $`\kappa=\mathrm{kaJ}(j)`$. The source gives $`\lambda\le o\le\lambda+\eta`$ and $`\kappa\ge0`$. If the nearest selected even predecessor has positive distance $`d`$, put $`t=a_{\rm prev}/p^d`$. Such a distance is odd. If no predecessor exists, put $`d=0,t=0`$. The latter convention reproduces both vanishing minimum terms because $`B,o\ge0`$. The exact leftover in the *assigned* local comparison is

```math
 r=o-\kappa+\min(B,t)-\min(B+o,t)
   =\min(o,(B+o-t)_+)-\kappa .
```

This follows by considering $`t\le B`$, $`B<t<B+o`$ and $`t\ge B+o`$. Kink assigns each predecessor/distance pair injectively to a nonnegative summand of the even-top series $`M_n`$. There can be unused summands. For $`q(B,l,t)=\min(l,(B+l-t)_+)`$, monotonicity and the unit Lipschitz bound in $`l`$ give
```math
 q(B,\lambda,t)-\kappa\le r_+\le q(B,\lambda,t)+\eta.
```
Consequently $`v_y=q(B,\lambda,t)/\lambda`$ is a sufficient fraction of the leading allowance once the old $`\eta`$ budget is retained. The independent index $`j`$ disappears from this upper proxy. In case E, the proof adds a lower estimate for $`g_U+dl`$, the comparison above, and an estimate on the remaining terms of $`{\rm Pr}`$, $`t_v`$, $`D_p`$ and the ceilings. Replacing the original $`N_v(\lambda+\eta)`$ by $`\lambda\sum_{y\ {\rm odd}}v_y+N_v\eta`$ preserves all of those inequalities. Relative to the published orbit inequality, add the nonnegative credit

## Transfer to the selected-family gap

1. For an active central prime $`p<H`$, the retained tops partition by representatives in $`V=\mathrm{VV}(p)`$. In the definition of gain the active contribution is the maximum of $`g_U-{\rm Pr}`$, $`g_0`$ and zero. The complementary representatives contribute $`\max(g_0,0)`$. Thus these terms can only improve the upper bound on the gap. The $`\tau`$ term includes the ceiling, $`D_p`$, and the constant 1; the floor budget pays for the $`dl`$ and $`{\rm ep}`$ terms. Summing the strengthened orbit inequality over $`j`$ therefore adds $`m\sum_{t_0\in V}c_{p,t_0}`$ to the left-hand side. At an inactive central prime $`p<H`$, the retained set is empty. Signal primes, primes $`p\ge H`$, and the low-central case retain the original treatment and receive zero credit. [Gain and retained-set definitions](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L353-L366) · [Branches and local summation](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/LogarithmicSums/NotSingularOfCentral.lean#L638-L728).

```math
 \Theta_v=\sum_{p\in S}\frac{\log p}{N}

```
The four $`p`$-independent residuals of the high-central price give at most $`g b_{\rm scale}/H`$ per retained top. ## Prime-power thresholds and the bulk rule

Here the family upper bound on $`b_y`$ matters. On the selected weights write
```math
 A\le a_{\rm prev}\le A(1+\alpha),\quad
 B_0\le B\le B_0(1+\beta),\quad
 \alpha=(k x^{A_{\rm const}/2})^{-1},\quad
 \beta=N_\star x^\epsilon L_a/k .
```
The family identifies its upper endpoint, and $`\rho_B=4\beta+\text{nonnegative term}\le x^{-2}`$ under the matched windows. The primitive InWin condition by itself has only the lower bound on $`B`$. [InWin](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/LogarithmicSums/Conclusion1OfGainBoundAll.lean#L99-L103) · [Family upper endpoint](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/FamCore.lean#L123-L130) · [Matched local endpoint](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GlobalTheory/PowFourLtCeilThresholdPrime.lean#L1821-L1830) · [Band definition](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L56-L85) · [Window bound on the radius](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/PrimeClassLossHypothesesSatisfiable.lean#L12-L30).

* If $`d=1`$ and $`p\le H/(1+x^{-1}+\beta)`$, then $`t\ge A/p\ge B+\lambda`$; thus $`v_y=0`$.
* If $`d\ge3`$ and $`p^3\ge H(1+\alpha)`$, then $`t\le A(1+\alpha)/p^3\le B`$; thus $`v_y=1`$. If $`d=0`$, $`v_y=1`$ at all primes.

In an even cycle, a positive odd predecessor distance is 1 or at least 3. Therefore outside the two strips $`p\le[H(1+\alpha)]^{1/3}`$ and $`H/(1+x^{-1}+\beta)<p<H`$, $`v_y`$ equals the indicator that the immediately preceding cyclic position is absent from the selected tops. Let $`\Theta_{\rm unpaired}`$ use that indicator in the definition of $`\Theta_v`$, restricted throughout to selected odd positions. Every prime has at most $`N`$ retained tops. Counting all integers in each strip and bounding $`\log p\le\log H`$ gives the uniform finite inequality
```math
 \frac{|\Theta_v-\Theta_{\rm unpaired}|}{H}
 \le \log H\left(\frac{[H(1+\alpha)]^{1/3}+2}{H}
                      +x^{-1}+\beta\right).
```
For fixed $`0<\epsilon\le1`$ along the large windows, $`\log H=x^{\epsilon/3}`$, $`0\le\alpha\le1`$ eventually, and $`\beta\le1/(4x^2)`$, making this bound $`o(1)`$. No assumption on prime independence or parity balance enters. The value or limit of $`\Theta_{\rm unpaired}/H`$ for the selected witnesses still requires information on their retained cyclic patterns. Unused even-top series terms and the other branch of the gain maximum can lower the actual deficit further.

On actual good case-E orbits the no-predecessor branch is eliminated. Write $`L_{\rm tile}=(6r+1)u`$, $`u=\lceil1/\delta_1\rceil\ge1`$, $`r=r_G\ge1`$. The numerical hypotheses and candidacy give $`n'\ge x^{\epsilon/4}\ge3G_p`$, while $`G_p=(M_0+5)L_{\rm tile}`$. There is at least one complete tile. Applying the good-orbit tile condition to tile index zero produces a marked tile. In its cell numbered 2 the selected set contains one even cyclic position. Every odd position can reach that even position by going backwards between 1 and $`n'`$ steps. Thus $`d\ne0`$ there. On these orbits the binary residual counts exactly the odd selected positions with $`d\ge3`$; the $`d=0`$ control tests the more general local comparison. Run the following in this directory. The second line uses a Lean 4.33.1 toolchain and the Mathlib revision in the original manifest, with its cache already available.

    python3 check_transport.py
    python3 check_prime_window_lean.py --toolchain /path/to/lean-4.33.1 --mathlib /path/to/pinned-mathlib

The rational controls check 8,125 scalar instances and 3,472 prime-window configurations. All 3,248 outside the two strips agree with the adjacency indicator. At $`p=983`$, $`d=1`$ gives 0 and $`d=3`$ gives 1. Removing the family upper bound and taking $`B=2B_0`$ at $`p=983,d=1`$ gives 1. These examples control overgeneralizations of the finite cutoff rule. The synthetic integers are not a chosen proof family. See [the controls](check_transport.py) and [their output](transport_results.json).

The two sufficient prime-power inequalities are implemented separately in [PrimeChargeWindow.lean](PrimeChargeWindow.lean). Their proof concerns real variables and natural exponents. The full transport from the pinned orbit theorem into the global gap and the asymptotic strip summation are mathematical derivations in this note. A replay of those two scalar inequalities does not replay the full formalization or strengthen its stated endpoints.
