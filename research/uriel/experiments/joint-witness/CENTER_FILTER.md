# Computing the near-sets when the centers are given

For the primitive character of conductor one, the actual auxiliary slots are 2, 3 and 7. This post-registration experiment supplies five rational $p$-adic integer centers for each slot and computes the finite filter in the source at $Z=64$. Only the slot indices come from the conductor-one calculation. The original proof obtains centers in a normed field from an existential `ABound` witness with a cohomological estimate. The rational centers here have no demonstrated connection to the field of the experimental prime pair. [Character specialization](EXCEPTIONS.md) · [Original filter and union](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LayeredIsomorphisms/Exc.lean#L7-L31)

Let $h=\lfloor\log_p Z\rfloor$. For a supplied center $s=a/b$ with $p\nmid b$, the strict condition $|s-e|_p<p^{-h}$ is equivalent to $be\equiv a\pmod {p^{h+1}}$. Since $p^{h+1}>Z$, there is at most one positive representative in $[1,Z]$. An implementation with ordinary modular inversion computes this representative. A second implementation enumerates every integer in the closed interval and calculates the norm by repeated division. They agreed for each supplied center and in 406 additional strict and non-strict comparisons around prime-power cutoffs. [Near-set uniqueness](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LayeredIsomorphisms/Near1.lean#L6-L43) · [Implementation](center_filter.py)

The union contains **seven integers** from fifteen centers. At 2, centers 1 and 129 give the same integer, while 65 gives none. The closed endpoint 64 appears in all three components. At 7, center 7 captures only 7 under the strict inequality. The integer 56 lies at exact distance $7^{-2}$, so weakening the comparison also captures 56 for this center. The overlap with the separate center 56 masks this change at the level of the union. The per-center outputs expose it. [Center-by-center output](results/center_filter.json)

To test the resulting union on a continuum, use the general window lemma at $C_t=1$, $\eta=1/128$ and $X=64$, giving $Z=\lfloor X^{C_t}\rfloor=64$. These parameters exercise the selector at its general interface. The character application uses $C_t=401$. With the supplied cap $R=15$, $J=2^{R+2}(C_t/\eta)^R=2^{122}$. The manually chosen $Y=2$ is in range. For a positive exception $e$, the forbidden interval for $x$ is exactly $[e,e^{128})$. None intersects $[2,4]$. On the alternative $[8,64]$, the point $x=8$ and exception $e=7$ violate goodness. This checks the full intervals with integer comparisons. It supplies no evidence about the actual Sha lengths, the slots' true caps or the admissible scale of the starting arithmetic field.
