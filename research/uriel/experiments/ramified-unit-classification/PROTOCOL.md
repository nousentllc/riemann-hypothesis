# Full valuation shell classification

## Test and failure conditions

For each positive scale, compare all unit targets with the square and companion representatives. A third normalized count at a resolving or later depth would falsify uniformity within the proposed two residue classes. A positive nonsquare example distinguishes a mass class from a unit square class. The shell theorem requires a value for every original target at valuation `4k`, witnesses realizing both values in each admissible family, and a proof that the two values differ.

The finite experiment visits every visible bin on the first three shells at the listed depths. It checks the unresolved predecessor, both coefficient parities, the small positive counts used in the lift and the squares modulo four. It counts all (2^{6d}) tuples in each quotient by convolution. Independent finite verification supplies controls and anchors. The kernel proof handles every depth and every unit.

## Proof obligations

1. Reduce an actual integral unit and its inverse to prove exhaustiveness of the two residual classes.
2. Account for every target at residual positive exponents zero and one, with the primitive and divisible parts distinguished. Propagate the comparison through the exact descent.
3. Check the count on both coefficient children of every late odd-depth ball. Use equality with the original square-centered ball for the sharp predecessor.
4. Identify the exact original valuation shell and transport the ratios through the pinned regular-fiber estimate on the same lattice and Haar normalization.
5. Check that the two witnesses can be chosen within the original valuation ring and that the two resulting masses are distinct.
