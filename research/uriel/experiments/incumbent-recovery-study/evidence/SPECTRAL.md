# An observable for a fixed retained set

In a dyadic coset of length \(2m\), use the least numeric label \(b\) as origin and a cyclic generator \(s\) for that coset. Let \(e(t)=1_T(b+2ts)\) and \(o(t)=1_T(b+(2t+1)s)\). For the generating direction \(h=(2r+1)s\), forward and reverse pair counts are \(J(r)=\sum_t e(t)o(t+r)\) and \(J(-r-1)\). These are direct functions of the same retained set. Label and cyclic coordinate can differ.

Their shared mean is \(\mu=|e||o|/m\), by counting each ordered pair once. For the unnormalized cyclic DFT, the transform of the forward count is \(\overline{\widehat e}\,\widehat o\). Orthogonality gives the centered variance \(\sigma^2=m^{-2}\sum_{\xi\ne0}|\widehat e(\xi)\widehat o(\xi)|^2\). A second-moment bound for a direction \(r\) chosen uniformly within this coset's generators gives \(\Pr(J(r)\le t)\le\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+(\mu-t)^2)\) whenever \(t<\mu\). This follows by applying Markov's inequality to \((\mu-J+a)^2\) and minimizing over \(a\ge0\). The zero-variance case follows directly. Neither the experimental mask nor the proof's original mask is resampled in this direction average.

For any compatible conductor-one incumbent, use the predecessor study's two-branch bound on the prime interior. Its local allowance minus its lower bound on the actual gap is at most \(\lambda[O-(x+1)J]_++o(N_v\lambda)\), where \(O\) counts retained odd positions. The outside-band signed deficit is bounded below up to an aggregate \(o(U)\). Summing and enlarging the nonnegative interior sum to all high inert directions gives
\[
 \mathcal R_x\le\frac{1}{2nN}\sum_{d}\sum_v [O_{dv}-(x+1)J_{dv}]_++o(1).
\]
The weight change uses the prior uniform estimate for each moving-host direction and polynomial group order. The trivial direction contributes zero. For given \(T\), the sum can be evaluated exactly. Its positive terms occur below \(t=O/(x+1)\); multiplying the preceding per-coset fraction by \(O\) bounds their average. This certificate is sufficient. A reverse first hit can defeat the fallback even if no forward pair exists. ## Independent experimental readings

The reference implementation counts the exact histogram of differences by adding one per ordered occupied endpoint pair. It verifies direct traversal for all 256 eight-point masks and validates the complex Fourier inversion and Parseval identity to absolute tolerance \(10^{-7}\) for all panels. Fractions in the JSON, including the variances and probability bounds, are computed from the integer histograms. The Fourier comparison uses floating point. At 65,536 points each of the three predeclared seeds produced 8,192 doubly empty directions and 12,288 forward-empty directions out of 16,384 on the structured half. The control with the same numbers of odd and even points had zero of each. With seed 17 both had mean 16, but their exact forward variances were \(6393371/8192\) and \(123029/8192\).

Any estimator of the fraction of doubly empty directions which uses only the shared tuple (total count, counts on both children, mean directed pair count) must incur absolute error at least \(1/4\) on one of these two concrete masks. The triangle inequality proves this because the true fractions differ by \(1/2\). This compares explicitly identified finite summaries. It does not claim the two masks have identical full arithmetic certificates, or that these large-scale numerical trials alone prove nonnecessity under the full selection rules. ## Corpus connection

Fraser and Rakhmonov study exceptional directions for projections of finite-field point sets. Their use of Fourier moments to quantify incidences motivated testing variation over directions rather than merely the total number of pairs. Section 2.1 gives the Fourier framework and section 8 treats incidence estimates. The cited local PDF is arXiv:2503.15072v1, SHA-256 24519a7fbb8a3a09ffdceec71782c26002cf93ec28d754a83d18ac7b0ce9a3bb.
