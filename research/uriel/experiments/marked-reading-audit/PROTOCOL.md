# Can two marks replace the removal of six incompatible settings?

Frozen analysis plan, 24 November 2026. Parent study [After the decisive reading](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/tree/03854270b77458b2b8f30e74049c142d9bb3c3a1/research/uriel/experiments/forced-reading-audit).

## Intervention and estimand

Give the local gpt-oss:20b reader both meter readings before it answers. Present each of 24 new records three ways in separate contexts. U (unmarked) shows all eight switch settings, M (marked) keeps all eight and flags precisely the two consistent with both readings, and F (filtered) displays only those two. Use the same legend, row order, fields, task, model configuration, schema allowing all eight IDs, and seed within each triple. The marks are truthful and supplied by an exact calculation. M versus U isolates the explicit eligibility cue at constant row count. M versus F compares two ways of supplying eligibility. It changes both exclusion and the visual encoding. This design does not isolate the pure cost of additional tokens or identify a private reasoning algorithm.

Six newly constructed layouts form the blocks. Each has eight settings, one fixed first reading, high and low replies on a decisive meter, and high and low replies on a different meter whose two surviving settings disagree about the target. Either reading in isolation must admit both target answers. Each joint intersection must have two settings. The decisive twins have opposite verdicts. The ambiguous twins both require U but have disjoint certificates. Require three layouts with each orientation of the high decisive reading. No selected hidden state or extra meter lookup. Run once per displayed record, balance the six display orders within the four record types, and archive actual requests and visible outputs. No retries of dispatched inferences. Count transport failures, truncations, unparsable answers and invalid certificates in their original denominators. Keep all model inference on the local machine with direct paid API cost zero.

## Scores and primary comparison

Certification requires the warranted verdict plus exactly the two distinct compatible IDs, in either order. Track exact witnesses and warranted verdict separately. The primary unit is the layout: an arm succeeds if it certifies both decisive replies. Compare M to F by both-pass, M-only, F-only, and neither. Report M minus F as a count and a proportion out of six. For the discordant layout directions give the exact two-sided conditional sign test and conditional 95% Clopper–Pearson interval (undefined for zero discordances). The sign test uses the fair independent-direction reference model, not a claim of random sampling of circuits. U versus M and U versus F are secondary comparisons. Do not use a failure to reject the sign null to infer equivalence.

Report certified decisive and ambiguous records out of twelve each, all records out of 24, complete ambiguous twins and complete quartets out of six, unsupported definitive claims on genuinely mixed cases, and simple exact-set versus warranted-verdict errors. Report M/F case-level agreement and crossovers even if aggregate scores tie. A per-record match means identical binary certification outcomes across all 24. A tie in six-layout primary totals can coexist with opposite wins or different certificates. Joint success on each case is a stronger property than joint failure. No design-based independence claim for the 24 records; models, seeds per case and layouts are fixed. No formal noninferiority or population equivalence threshold is justified by this pilot.

## Falsifiable predictions and controls

1. If an eligibility cue is sufficient on this panel, M should recover F's successes on the primary endpoint with limited losses on the mixed twins, and both should improve on U. Report counts and identities even if the result is uneven.
2. If visible excluded settings still interfere after marking, F should have unique successes relative to M; name whether the defect is an excluded ID, an omitted ID, a mistaken verdict on the submitted set, or a missing answer.
3. If high scores primarily reflect a memorized sign or one-sided meter shortcut, the mixed twins and inverse decisive layouts expose it. A policy that repeats the same IDs cannot certify both mixed replies even if it correctly answers U twice. A perfect public algorithm forms the intersection then checks unanimity. For M and F, reading the two marked or visible rows and checking their labels is sufficient. A claim about changing the model's underlying competence requires different evidence.

Independently recalculate scores from public prompt rows and response text. For any missing completions, report sharp optimistic/pessimistic bounds for each comparison on the primary count, retaining the registered missing-as-failure estimate. Show exact witness mistakes on representative nontrivial cases and publish the full ledger so their frequency is inspectable. The study concerns the evidence needed to support a conclusion; it adds no Lean theorem, model-training result, or statement about the Riemann Hypothesis.
