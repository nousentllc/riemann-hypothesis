# Fixed-first two-witness experiment, 23 November 2026

Freeze the target as the closed real interval [8,12] before choosing any exceptional sets, scales or orientation. This tests the active-weight interface recovered in PR 18, not the actual centers of a number field. The prior single-set fill result, including the strict floor crossover, was known when designing these controls.

1. Set the toy cutoff t(x)=floor(x^2), allowance x, with strict activity 1<=m<t(x). Use square coordinates to compare all real points exactly. Enumerate every ordered pair consisting of two subsets of {4,8,9,10,11,12,32,63,64,80,99,120,143,144}, each of size at most two, including the empty list. Select only scenarios with a successful left endpoint for the first list and successful right endpoint for the second. Independently evaluate all event points and intervening open cells on the prescribed target, and compare with a closed-form prediction based on extreme obstructing labels. Distinguish (i) one fixed list covers the whole interval, (ii) pointwise availability of at least one list, (iii) certifiability by an ordered left-to-right handoff, and (iv) existence of a point where both pass. Count separately positive-length hole, endpoint/equality, and failure despite equal list sizes or common labels. Counts weight constructed configurations only.
2. Test the precise distinction between identical lists, equality of their relevant labels, directional inclusion, and mere nonempty intersection. Check at both endpoints if needed. Include cases in which disjoint lists patch, intersecting lists fail to patch, both individual lists fail to cover but a complete patch exists, and coverage holds although there is no point where both tests pass. Derive the exact worst missing component and a minimal hand-checkable witness based on its two labels.
3. Compare against closed domain clipping: first witness available only up to c1, second only from c2 onwards. Freeze four variants of (c1,c2): (12,8), (10,10), (11,9), and (9,11). Verify pointwise on all event points and open cells. Test strict and reversed comparisons at the handoff. Auxiliary geometry at a different crossover may distinguish general pointwise coverage from a single ordered handoff. No inference that either availability interval occurs at an original arithmetic scale.
4. Express the same conclusions for positive general exponents Ct>eta, with the floor convention and the cutoff order explicit. Explore whether integer discretization weakens the prior strict crossover for monotonic endpoint-oriented failures. Any such improvement is a follow-up identified as arising after the baseline protocol. The mathematical output must distinguish identity of a field witness, local numerical adequacy of a test, and the scalar statement about a fixed observable on compatible hosts.
