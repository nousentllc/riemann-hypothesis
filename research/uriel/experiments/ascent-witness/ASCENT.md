# Class group ascent at 3

Let $K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{3^{k+1}})$ and $E=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{3^{k+2}})$, realized as consecutive subfields of the source's chosen algebraic closure. Write $C_E$ for the ideal class group and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)$. The adjacent-field check identifies $G$ as cyclic of order three. [Field and ramification proofs](NextLayer.lean)

## The first cubic step

For $E=\mathbf Q(\zeta_9)$, Lean proves that every ideal in its ring of integers is principal. Its degree is 6, its discriminant is $-19683$, and it has three complex places. These give a Minkowski bound strictly below 5. Among prime ideals small enough to represent a class, a prime above 2 would have norm $2^6=64$ by the order of 2 modulo 9. Every prime above 3 is generated by $\zeta_9-1$. This gives class number 1 unconditionally in the original tower's own field model. Consequently its map on coinvariants is injective already at this first extension. The previous experiment's independent finite calculation used the same mathematical bound. ## Following the actual class norm

A relative automorphism acts by taking the class of an ideal to the class of its image. For a generator $s$ let $D_s(c)=s(c)/c$. The published ideal norm gives a homomorphism $N:C_E\to C_K$. Lean checks $N(s(c))=N(c)$ and constructs the induced norm from $C_E/\mathrm{im}(D_s)$ to $C_K$. This uses invariance of the relative ideal norm under algebra automorphisms. [Original class norm on integral ideals](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/NumberFieldTowers/IdealNormClassCongr.lean#L39-L46) · [Relative ideal norm invariance](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Norm/RelNorm.lean#L314-L327)

The classes fixed by every element of $G$ are exactly $\ker D_s$. For an endomorphism of a finite abelian group, its kernel and the quotient by its image have the same cardinality. The orbit count gives $|C_E|\equiv |C_E^G|\pmod 3$. Thus injectivity of the induced norm implies $|C_E^G|\mid |C_K|$ and propagates $3\nmid |C_K|$ up one layer. The Lean induction applies these statements to the genuine tower and yields the previous experiment's exact `TowerClassHyp`. **What the orbit count leaves open.** The equality $\ker N=\mathrm{im}(D_s)$ asserts that every class of trivial norm is a difference. Norm invariance proves the inclusion from right to left. The other inclusion uses global arithmetic. In the classical proof the single ramified prime forces injectivity of the sum of inertia maps in the cyclic class group exact sequence. Sharifi gives the resulting injectivity and its application to class numbers. A targeted search found the source's related Ferrero–Washington argument: its theorem `amb` gives an upper bound on the index of a subgroup generated by powers and differences. Its conclusion supplies neither the kernel equality nor the exact fixed-class divisibility required here. ## An alternative using unramified extensions

The source provides the Hilbert class field, its universal property for everywhere-unramified abelian extensions and the Artin isomorphism with the ideal class group. In particular, every finite everywhere-unramified abelian extension of the lower field has degree dividing its class number. With a lower class number prime to three, an everywhere-unramified cubic subextension is impossible. In a finite 3-group, every proper subgroup is contained in a normal subgroup of index three. For a candidate Galois 3-extension upstairs, applying this to inertia gives a specific degree-three quotient to test. The field-theoretic construction takes the fixed field of the cubes in the upstairs Hilbert class group. The inertia calculation makes its cubic fixed field everywhere unramified downstairs. The proof by contradiction and the induction on layers are assembled in the final module. ## What transfer recovers after the induction

For any finite Galois extension of number fields, extending the ideal class $N(c)$ back upstairs gives the product of all the conjugates of $c$. To prove this in Lean, take a nonzero representative ideal and raise it to the order of its ideal class. The resulting principal ideal reduces the product identity to the formula for the field norm. Positive powers distinguish ideals in a Dedekind domain. This generalizes the ideal-power step in the original proof from a particular cyclotomic indexing of conjugates to the actual relative Galois group. [Transfer proof](Transfer.lean) · [Original cyclotomic argument](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/PrimitiveFrobeniusFixed/XNormDiv.lean#L168-L195)

If the upstairs class number is prime to 3, cubing is injective. On fixed classes the product of the three conjugates is the cube, so the norm is injective on fixed classes. In the quotient by generator differences, all three conjugates have the same image. A class of trivial norm therefore has cube-trivial image in that quotient, which also has order prime to 3. Its image is the identity. This proves injectivity of the induced norm on coinvariants, including the reverse inclusion in the kernel equality above. The premise for these corollaries is supplied by the independent unramified-extension argument. [Class norm in both models](Transfer.lean) · [Independent induction](TowerResult.lean)

Running the two maps in the other order gives $N(\mathrm{ext}(d))=d^3$ downstairs. The lower class number is also prime to three, so every downstairs class is a cube. It follows that the induced norm on coinvariants is bijective at every layer and the number of fixed classes upstairs is exactly the full class number downstairs. This equality of cardinalities leaves the actual class numbers at general layers undetermined.
