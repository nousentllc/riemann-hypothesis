# Every consecutive cubic layer

Let $F_n$ be the source field `Fn (CyclotomicField 3 ℚ) Ω 3 n`. For every $n\ge1$, consider the actual inclusion $F_n\hookrightarrow F_{n+1}$ used by the source to define its norm on ideal classes. The earlier experiment identified these fields with the corresponding rational cyclotomic fields. This experiment keeps their inclusion throughout the degree, Galois-group and ramification arguments. | Question for each step | Kernel-checked result |
|---|---|
| What is the extension? | Its degree is 3 and it is Galois with cyclic group of order 3. |
| Which primes ramify? | Exactly one nonzero prime ideal of $F_n$ ramifies. It is the unique prime over 3. Every other nonzero prime is unramified. |
| What happens over that prime? | There is exactly one prime upstairs, with relative ramification index 3 and relative residue degree 1. |
| Can that prime contribute a nontrivial ideal class? | The prime over 3 at every layer is generated by $\zeta_{3^n}-1$, so its ideal class is trivial. |
| Can a real place contribute ramification? | Each field is totally complex. |

The index $k$ in `NextLayer.lean` starts at zero, with $n=k+1$. The absolute degrees are $2\cdot3^k$ and $2\cdot3^{k+1}$. The tower degree formula gives relative degree 3. For the prime over 3, the absolute ramification indices have this same ratio, and both absolute residue degrees are 1. The tower formulas give the relative invariants and the number of relative primes. For every rational prime $q\ne3$, the upstairs absolute ramification index is 1. Multiplicativity forces the relative index to equal 1. The proof then recovers the rational prime below any nonzero base prime, establishing the all-primes statement. The branch of the argument using the ambiguous class number formula still needs information about **all ideal classes fixed by the Galois group**. The computation of the unique ramified prime determines one principal class. In general, an automorphism can carry an ideal to a different ideal in the same class, so a fixed class need not come with a fixed ideal representative. Counting ramified primes alone does not supply the required divisibility of the number of fixed classes. ## An alternative descent through inertia

Let $F=F_n$, $E=F_{n+1}$, and let $U/E$ be everywhere unramified, with $U/F$ Galois. Suppose $G=\operatorname{Gal}(U/F)$ is a 3-group with more than three elements. Then there is an intermediate field $M$ such that $M/F$ is an everywhere-unramified abelian extension of degree three. This statement is the other route into the class-number argument. It uses the group lemma that each proper subgroup of a finite 3-group is contained in a normal subgroup of index three. [Inertia descent](InertiaDescent.lean)

To see the construction, choose one prime of $U$ over the unique ramified prime of $F$. Its inertia subgroup $I$ has order three. The reason is local: the ramification index from $E$ to $U$ is one, and the index from $F$ to $E$ is three. At every other finite prime the inertia subgroup has order one. Choose a normal subgroup $H$ of index three containing $I$. All other primes of $U$ above that first prime have conjugate inertia, and normality keeps each conjugate inside $H$. Thus $H$ contains all finite inertia. The restriction of inertia onto $M=U^H$ is surjective. Since $H$ fixes $M$, each restricted inertia group is trivial. The base is totally complex, so all infinite places also satisfy the original unramifiedness predicate. The Galois correspondence gives $[M:F]=3$, and a group of order three is cyclic. The Lean statement exposes the normal-subgroup selector as an explicit hypothesis, discharged by the finite 3-group lemma in the experiment. The application to one layer requires a nontrivial unramified $U/E$ that is also Galois with 3-power degree over $F$. The local descent theorem proves the consequence of having such a $U$. The [elementary Hilbert class field construction](UPSTAIRS.md) provides this extension. The [combined theorem](TowerResult.lean) applies the descent and closes the induction.
