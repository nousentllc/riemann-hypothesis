# Where the factor of two enters the anisotropic Siegel–Weil formula

**Source investigation · 24 November 2026 · Nous / Uriel**

For a test vector Φ, write IΦ for the theta sum averaged over the unitary quotient, EΦ for the Eisenstein family built from its inducing section, and x for a point on the doubled group. Write d for the dimension of the anisotropic member of the unitary pair (skew-Hermitian in the lift interface, Hermitian in the Gram-density coordinates) and n for the number of vectors in the Gram matrix. The Lean datum often uses `d.m` and `d.n` for these two numbers. With the theorem’s measure and character conventions, the continued family has special value

$$
 E_Φ(s_0,x)=c_{d,n}IΦ(x),\qquad s_0=(d-n)/2,\qquad
 c_{d,n}=\begin{cases}1&n<d,\\2&d\le n.\end{cases}
$$

The outer lift statement applies this to boxed polynomial data over a CM extension, a definite target at the stated signature, compatible Weil and doubling data, an admissible character, and a normalized Haar mean. It holds at all x. A particular test can have value zero on both sides. The proof supplies three explanations depending on rank. Positive codimension gives a normalized product of local densities with coefficient one. Equal rank gives an index-two global norm subgroup and calibrates the product against twice the mean. When n exceeds d the proof inherits that calibration by deleting one vector at a time. The arithmetic-window route studied previously has n≥1 and d≥2n. It uses the first case even when d=2n. ## 1. Start with the literal zero-frequency term

For a rational Fourier index T and a translate x, unfolding the theta coefficient gives the normalized average of a sum over rational d-by-n matrices X. In the chosen Fourier coordinates the surviving matrices satisfy
```math
 \mathrm{Gram}(X)=-2T .
```
The unipotent integral has mass one and character orthogonality enforces this condition. A different integral, over the unitary fundamental domain, is divided by its finite positive volume. At T=0 every column has norm zero. Definiteness at the chosen infinite place and injectivity of the field embedding give anisotropy over the base field. Consequently every column is zero. The single remaining summand evaluates the transformed test vector at zero and is independent of the averaging variable. Thus
```math
  \mathrm{FC}_0(IΦ)(x)=\mathrm{sec}_0Φ(x).
```
This last display composes the indicated declarations. The declaration evaluating the section at zero alone proves only the final identification. This is a useful check on any proposed explanation of the multiplier. The same zero matrix survives at every rank, and the value of the test there can itself be zero. Once the full theorem is known, it gives the corresponding Eisenstein constant coefficient by multiplying this expression by c. Counting zero matrices cannot distinguish the two cases. ## 2. Fewer vectors than dimensions. How one is forced

**Which Eisenstein term survives?** First take an invertible Fourier index T. Write a rational representative as a block matrix with lower blocks C,D. If C is invertible its class belongs to the open Weyl orbit, with additive coordinate C⁻¹D. If C is singular, choose a nonzero u in its kernel and y with yᵀTu=1. The source constructs a shift whose conjugate preserves the section and whose Fourier phase is −1. Invariance of the measure gives J=−J for the integrated orbit contribution, so J=0. This argument uses the nontrivial Fourier character. At T=0 the same shift has phase +1. Our exact two-by-two example over ℚ(i) realizes both signs with the same matrices. The integer 2 in the auxiliary trace tr(Tβ)=2 is used to arrange a phase and has a different role from the global normalization. ## 3. Equal rank. A global index survives the comparison

At n=d, s₀=0 is the boundary of the preceding Euler-product argument. Write ηᵥ=±1 at an unramified place of norm qᵥ. The borderline factor at zero is 1−ηᵥ/qᵥ. In the source’s equal-rank product calculation the analytic value K(0), the exceptional set S, the Euler limit Λ and the complementary normalized product P satisfy
$$
 K(0)\prod_{v\in S}(1-\eta_v/q_v)=\Lambda P .
$$
This equality isolates a single borderline factor family. Its right side has no automatic value of two. The source must relate that product to a *globally* normalized orbital mean. [WqMain 66–88](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/AutomorphicKernels/SiegelWeilFormula/WhittakerEqualRank/WqMain.lean#L66-L88) [Equal-rank replay and its hypotheses](verification/README.md)

For the rank-one base over the quadratic extension E/F, the proof identifies an open subgroup of the idele group,
```math
 H=F^\times N(\mathbb A_E^\times)
  =\ker\big(\mathrm{normSign}:\mathbb A_F^\times\longrightarrow\{+1,-1\}\big).
```
Here an idele specifies a compatible family of invertible local elements; multiplication by one common rational element is allowed. The kernel identification is a global theorem. A further existence theorem constructs an idele outside H by choosing obstructed local data and patching the other components. Thus the character takes −1 somewhere. Together with its value +1 at the identity, this gives surjectivity and index two. A homomorphism into a two-element group need not have index-two kernel. We kernel-checked both the conditional implication and the trivial-character control, whose kernel has index one. The source proves existence of the obstructed idele, but this audit did not extract a numerical idele for the window datum. **A representative you can check by hand.** For E=ℚ(i), take the idele A with component −1 at the real place and 1 at every finite prime. Suppose A=r N(x) for a single rational r. At infinity all nonzero complex norms are positive, so r<0. For every p≡3 mod 4 the valuation of a nonzero local norm a²+b² is even. Indeed, after removing the smaller coefficient valuation, a sum of two squares with at least one unit cannot vanish modulo such a p. Thus every such prime has even valuation in r. By rational prime factorization the odd unit u=r/2^ord₂(r) must now be 3 modulo 4: the sign contributes −1, primes 1 modulo 4 contribute 1, and all primes 3 modulo 4 occur with even exponents, including in the denominator. At 2, however, the odd unit of a nonzero norm from ℚ₂(i) is 1 modulo 4. After removing a square power of two, unequal coefficient valuations give a sum 1 modulo 4; if both coefficients are odd, their squares sum to 2 modulo 8. Hence the odd part of 1/r, which must be a norm at 2, is simultaneously 3 and 1 modulo 4. This contradiction exhibits an element of the second coset. It illustrates the need for one *common* rational multiplier. It proves an obstruction for this example; the source supplies the global kernel description and exact index for its full field hypotheses.

The measure calculation uses translates of an idele fundamental domain. The two cosets have equal volume. The same Tate asymptotic is applied once on H and once on the full group. With p the finite local correction, v the test volume, V a positive finite volume from the Tate formula, and L the nonnegative real form of Λ, uniqueness of the limit yields
$$
  (L p)(vV/2)=\kappa(pvV).
$$

Therefore the Whittaker product with factor Λ equals **twice the theta mean**. There is one global index in the rank-one calculation. Peeling a represented one-by-one block transfers the same equal-rank normalization to larger frames. The final comparison is an identity of products valid for complex tests, including vanishing ones. In the notation of the proof, 2 mean = g∞ Λ ∏ᵥ(gᵥ/Dᵥ), with Dᵥ=1−ηᵥ/qᵥ in the equal-rank normalization. The product at a chosen complex test may vanish. Only nonzero elementary factors are inverted. ## 4. More vectors than dimensions. The two actual orbit terms

If d<n, no d-by-n matrix can have a nonsingular n-by-n Gram matrix. Nonsingular theta coefficients now all vanish. Matching those zeros cannot detect a multiplier. The proof inducts on n−d, starting from the equal-rank value two. After a rational basis change, a singular index has a designated zero row and column. Anisotropy forces the corresponding column of every representing X to be zero. Deleting it and inserting it back gives inverse maps of the theta fibers. The lower-rank special parameter is s₀+½. This identifies the theta quantity that the induction hypothesis controls.
