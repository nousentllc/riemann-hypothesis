# Two choices of center for the same matrix

The field-specific input is the assertion that the source module $X_P$ at $\ell=3$ over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ is trivial. With this input, one can keep the presentation, action, lift, root and cyclotomic unit fixed and still obtain opposite membership decisions for weight 1 at $Z=64$. The argument applies to any $u\in\mathbb Z_3$, and in particular to the $u$ satisfying the tower's `IsLevel` condition. Fix one generating slot, $R=1$, so $j=\lfloor\log_3 1\rfloor+3=3$. Use the surjection $\pi:\mathbb Z_3\to X_P$ that sends its single formal generator to zero. If $A$ is the Galois action from the proof, the zero matrix $S=[0]$ obeys $\pi S=A^{27}\pi$. The cokernel on the subsingleton module has length zero, which also verifies the inequality required by the original matrix-lift theorem. Extending scalars preserves the zero matrix, its characteristic polynomial is $T$, and its root multiset contains zero once. At every scalar $a$, the determinant $\det(aS-I)$ equals $-1$. The field has cyclotomic level $c=1$. For this root $z=0$, the source's center inequality
$$3^{-(j+c)}|s-e|_3\le |(u^{3^j})^e z-1|_3$$
reduces to $|s-e|_3/81\le1$ for every natural $e$. Every $s\in\mathbb Z_3$ satisfies this because $s-e\in\mathbb Z_3$. Thus the intersection of closed balls permits every integral center. At the strict cutoff $1/27$, $s=1$ captures weight 1 by distance zero and $s=0$ misses it by distance one. Both choices satisfy all the ball inequalities for this same root.
