# Arithmetic of the conductor-one slot 3

The field gives a stronger answer than the uniform bound over abelian fields. In this cyclotomic tower the specialized module `XP` is zero. Consequently the arithmetic bound admits either center 0 or center 1 with the same cap and the same base field. At cutoff 64 the first choice gives the empty set and the second gives exactly $\{1\}$. The following argument identifies the extra field-specific input, the source declarations that use it, and the freedom in building a witness.

## An exact finite field check

Put $F_n=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{3^n})$ for $n\ge 1$. Our standard-library computation certifies $h(F_1)=h(F_2)=1$. It uses the usual cyclotomic integer basis and Minkowski's bound. For $d=[F_n:\mathbf Q]$, the field is totally imaginary and every class has an integral ideal with norm at most $(4/\pi)^{d/2}d!\sqrt{|D|}/d^d$. For $m=3^{n-1}$ the polynomial is $X^{2m}+X^m+1$ and the absolute discriminant of the integral basis is $m^{2m}3^m$, by the derivative norm. The program uses exact rational upper bounds on the Minkowski expression. [Minkowski's class representative theorem](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/ClassNumber.lean#L74-L85) · [Finite arithmetic program](field_checks.py) · [Recorded certificates](results/field_checks.json)

| Field | Degree | Absolute discriminant | Largest norm to check | Possible norms up to the bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| $\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$ | 2 | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| $\mathbf Q(\zeta_9)$ | 6 | 19,683 | 4 | 1, 3 |

For the second field the program checked irreducibility of $X^6+X^3+1$ modulo 2 by trial division by every monic polynomial of degrees 1 through 3. The unique prime above 2 has norm 64. Modulo 3 the polynomial is $(X-1)^6$. The element $1-\zeta_9$ has norm 3 and generates the unique norm-3 prime. Thus all the candidate ideals are principal. As an independent base check, reduced primitive quadratic forms give only $(1,1,1)$ for discriminant $-3$. The control at discriminant $-23$ returns three classes. Reproduce both field checks with `python3 -B field_checks.py` in this directory.

Lean also replayed the class-number-one result on the literal type `CyclotomicField 3 ℚ` via Mathlib’s `three_pid`. Three further audited declarations prove that 3 does not divide its class group cardinality, that an element of order $3^k$ exists precisely for $k\le1$, and that the supremum defining the tower’s base level is 1. These checks use Mathlib alone and distinguish the bottom field from the all-layer input below. ## What extends to every layer

The finite calculations determine regularity of 3 from the definition $3\nmid h(\mathbf Q(\zeta_3))$. Yokoi's Theorem 3(ii) applies to every $F_n$ and gives $3\nmid h(F_n)$. One can also see the induction directly. From $F_n$ to $F_{n+1}$ the Galois group has order 3. Exactly one finite prime ramifies, with ramification index 3, and there are no real places. The ambiguous class number formula gives the fixed subgroup order $h(F_n)/[\mathcal O_{F_n}^{\times}:\mathcal O_{F_n}^{\times}\cap N F_{n+1}^{\times}]$, an integer divisor of $h(F_n)$. Counting orbits of size 1 or 3 on the upstairs class group shows that $h(F_{n+1})$ is congruent modulo 3 to this fixed subgroup order. This supplies every layer's prime-to-3 condition. Its justification is a published number-theoretic theorem and the argument above. The finite experiment covers two layers. [Yokoi, formula (1), Lemma 1 and Theorem 3(ii)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/20/1-2/20_1-2_411/_pdf)

## Why the zero module is the relevant module

1. Work in an algebraic closure $\Omega$ of $L=F_1$. The source field `Fn L Ω 3 n` adjoins $\mu_{3^n}$ to $L$ and is isomorphic over $L$ to $F_n$ for $n\ge2$. The separate Lean transport proof identifies the fields over the rationals, induces an isomorphism on their rings of integers and class groups, and proves equality of their class group cardinalities at every positive layer. Cubing is a bijection on the resulting finite class group by the prime-to-3 order. [Layer and class group equivalences](CyclotomicTransport.lean) Thus the quotient by cubes has cardinality $1=3^0$. Use `levelRk` with $r=0$ in every layer and `infRk` at $U=\top$, $N=1$. This gives `XRk 3 1 (cycU 3 ⊤) 0`. ## Two actual-field choices of center

The geometric cap in `IsTower` must be at least one. The source takes $R=\max(1,r)$, so $r=0$ permits $R=1$. At $L=F_1$ take $c=1$, witnessed by $\zeta_3$. There is no primitive ninth root in this quadratic field since the ninth cyclotomic field has degree 6. Set $R'=1$ and take the center in $\mathbf Q_3$. Since every left side $\operatorname{shaInv}$ above is zero, both inequalities in `ABound` hold for every choice of this one integral center. In particular, 0 and 1 are equally admissible while keeping the field, cap, $c$, and number of centers fixed. This uses arithmetic information about this field to discharge the cohomological premises. [Original cap choice](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/IsAlgClosureRat.lean#L98-L110) · [Both bounds in ABound](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LayeredIsomorphisms/SecondBound.lean#L11-L30)

The freedom is visible even in the determinant argument. Present the zero module using one generator and the zero surjection $\mathbf Z_3\to0$. A one-by-one zero matrix on the free module lifts any endomorphism of the zero quotient. Its only eigenvalue is $z=0$. For this eigenvalue the right side of the source's ball condition is $\lVert -1\rVert_3=1$ at every weight. Every $s\in\mathbf Z_3$ meets it, since $3^{-(j+c)}\lVert s-e\rVert_3\le1$. Thus specifying a field and even an admissible lift does not select a unique center in this example. The original argument is free to use another lift. At $Z=64$ the strict source filter for these integral centers is $e\equiv s\pmod {81}$ for $1\le e\le 64$. Center 0 yields no exceptional integer. For any specified $a$ in this interval, center $a$ yields precisely $\{a\}$. In particular a positive and a negative membership answer for $e=1$ both extend to legitimate witnesses of the arithmetic bound. The stronger arithmetic conclusion is that *no exceptional weight is required to bound these specialized invariants*. The proof's particular existentially selected near-set has no exported identifier or residues with which to determine its membership.
