# What one can force in a slot-three witness

Fix the cutoff $Z=64$ and the integer $a=1$. The original `exc` theorem gives a finite exceptional set with an upper bound. The source body constructs one from the union of strict balls about a chosen list of centers. The conclusion hides the list. The following local theorems use the original definitions of `ABound`, `FirstBound`, `SecondBound` and `Tower.IsTower`. **An alternative cohomological witness can include 1.** Start with a tower for the field at a cap $R$ and the source's supplier of admissible bounds for that field. A surjection from $R$ generators extends to $R+1$ generators by ignoring the extra coordinate. Apply the supplier at both caps. In the first clause of the old bound, append 1 as a center, retain the root of unity and keep the away-from-centers inequality. Being far from every center in the longer list implies being far from every old center. The new bound's right side is larger. The separate second bound refers to the layer at $j_0(3,R+1)$, so take that clause from the supplier at the **new** cap. This works even at a jump of the integer logarithm. The resulting data satisfy the full original `ABound`. The theorem `named_filter` uses that same list in the strict filter from `exc`. The distance from its nominated center to 1 is zero. The proof checks the full `FirstBound` for the resulting set, including the cohomology bound outside it. The cap has increased by one. The general version names any $a\in[1,64]$, separately for each $a$. The conclusion is conditional on the original field's tower and bound supplier. These source inputs are traced above. There is no claim about membership of 1 in the earlier witness, or about the value of any individual cohomological length. [`tower_filter_at_one` and its construction](SlotThree.lean)

A weaker experiment enlarges an existing `FirstBound` exceptional set by inserting a prescribed integer and increasing its cap by one. Both the cap and $j_0$ give monotone upper bounds here. This construction acts only on the exported exceptional set. A negative control tried to turn the original existential statement directly into an assertion that its set contains 1. Lean rejected that substitution with a type mismatch. This control checks the interface. It does not establish independence of membership from every additional arithmetic hypothesis. [Checker output](results/formal.json)

**Exact cutoff.** The source uses $3^{-\lfloor\log_3 64\rfloor}=1/27$. For integer centers the strict inequality is equivalent to divisibility of the integer difference by 81. This remains true in every normed algebra over $\mathbb Q_3$ by its isometric coefficient embedding. A difference of 27 passes the weak test and fails the strict test. The source's determinant construction actually embeds a list of elements of $\mathbb Z_3$ into $\mathbb Q_3$. The first four base-three digits of each center determine its residue class modulo 81 and suffice to implement the finite filter. [Original coefficient field](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/AdditiveValuationNorms/ABoundOfDescends.lean#L36-L49) · [Kernel-checked cutoff and controls](SlotThree.lean)

## Reproduce the formal check

The executed run authenticated 36 earlier compiled objects and compiled two additional original modules, covering all 38 original sources in the union of the two import closures. Three individual kernel replays succeeded. Each of the 16 local theorem inventories reported `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Early drafts of the cutoff proof needed explicit casts between natural-number, integer and real numerals. The recorded source compiled without `sorry`. [Complete local results](results/formal.json)

Run from this directory with the pinned Lean v4.33.1 toolchain and Mathlib setup described in the [preceding experiment](../joint-witness/REPRODUCE.md). Use an empty work directory. ```
