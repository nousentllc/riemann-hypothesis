# Where the slot-3 centers come from

The field is explicit. The source's determinant construction also localizes its centers more sharply than the abstract statement suggests. It produces a list in the ordinary 3-adic integers, followed by their inclusion into the 3-adic rationals. At cutoff $Z=64$, the missing datum for the list is its image in $\mathbb Z/81\mathbb Z$. The actual window theorem then makes an additional existential selection of a cap, field model, bound and exceptional set. It exports a window with estimates. ## Fix the field, its cyclotomic level and the cutoff

For the character of conductor one, `Lchi ψ 3 = AbF.cyc (3*1)`. Take $L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$. The tower's `cZero` is the supremum of the exponents $c$ for which $L$ contains a primitive $3^c$-th root of unity. Specializing the definition gives $c=1$. A primitive cube root lies in $L$, and a primitive ninth root would generate an extension of degree $\varphi(9)=6$ inside a field of degree $\varphi(3)=2$. Any primitive root of higher 3-power order would give a primitive ninth root by taking a power. This is an independent field calculation. The proof's general `ABound` only requires *a* level with a primitive root. Write $R$ for a tower cap, $j=\lfloor\log_3 R\rfloor+3$, and $u$ for the cyclotomic character of the selected Galois element $\gamma$. The tower has $R\ge1$. The proof yields $v_3(u-1)=1$ and a surjection $\pi:\mathbb Z_3^R\twoheadrightarrow X_P$, where $X_P$ is the tower module at $N=3$. The bound on $R$ starts with an existential bound on the 3-ranks of class groups throughout a cyclotomic tower. The source takes $R=\max(1,r)$ in the exhibited tower construction. This gives neither a minimal generator count nor a numbered list of generators. ## Follow the witness through the proof body

1. **Choose a generator and lift its action.** `genLevel` obtains a unit in the principal subgroup of level $c$ outside the next subgroup, then chooses a Galois preimage $\gamma$. The endomorphism on $X_P$ is $A=\mathrm{xRep}(\gamma)$. At level $j$ the proof opens the existence of $\pi$ and chooses a preimage of $A^{3^j}\pi(e_k)$ for each coordinate vector $e_k$. These preimages form the columns of an $R\times R$ matrix $S$ over $\mathbb Z_3$. The relation is $\pi S=A^{3^j}\pi$. The proof supplies surjectivity and a determinant bound. Producing actual entries would require knowing the presentation and the action in it. 2. **Factor a specific determinant.** Over the normed algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_3$, the proof enumerates the roots $z_1,\ldots,z_R$ of the characteristic polynomial of the lifted matrix, with multiplicity. For every scalar $a$ it obtains $\det(aS-I)=\prod_i(az_i-1)$. The slots $i$ index roots of this *lift*. They need not correspond to distinct eigenvalues. [Characteristic roots and their enumeration](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/AdditiveValuationNorms/DetFactor.lean#L6-L35) · [Factorization in the normed extension](https://github.com/nousentllc/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/AdditiveValuationNorms/DetBound.lean#L9-L24)

3. **Choose a 3-adic center per root.** Put $b=u^{3^j}$. For a fixed root $z_i$ the proof gives the closed balls in $\mathbb Z_3$ centered at every natural number $n$, of radius $r_i(n)=3^{j+c}|b^n z_i-1|_3$. For each nonempty finite collection a center attaining the smallest radius belongs to all its balls. Compactness of $\mathbb Z_3$ yields a common point $s_i$ for the entire infinite collection. The source chooses one such point for each $i$. In particular
$$|s_i-n|_3\le 3^{j+c}|b^n z_i-1|_3\qquad(n\ge0).$$
The literal zero in the finite-intersection proof handles its empty family of constraints. Using the infinite intersection requires the compactness step at lines 20–21. At $Z=64$ the test is $|s_i-e|_3<3^{-3}$, equivalently $s_i\equiv e\pmod{81}$ for this integral construction. Each residue in $1,\ldots,64$ has a unique representative in the test range. Residues $0,65,\ldots,80$ have none. A single certified root with $|b^e z_i-1|_3 < 3^{-(j+c+3)}$ would force every valid choice in that root's ball intersection to satisfy the near-test at $e$. This follows by multiplying the strict inequality by $3^{j+c}$. It identifies useful finite arithmetic information even without computing a full 3-adic center. The traced construction supplies no such numerical residual for the field.

Two limiting cases clarify this criterion. If $z_i=1$, the ball at $n=0$ has radius zero, forcing $s_i=0$. This center hits no weight in $1,\ldots,64$. If $z_i=0$, all the radii are $3^{j+c}$ and any $s_i\in\mathbb Z_3$ satisfies these constraints. The argument does not establish that either eigenvalue occurs in the selected arithmetic matrix. These observations are deductions from the displayed inequality.

## What survives the last existential choices

The headline supplies the arithmetic tower bound to `Two.fixedIn`, then passes that theorem to `thmBab_of`. Inside the latter, $P(3,L,R)$ asserts the existence of *some* field model and *some* `ABound`. The cap is `Classical.choose` of $\exists R,P(3,L,R)$. The proof subsequently opens `Classical.choose_spec` to obtain its field model and `ABound` proof. The fixed-field tower establishes that such choices exist. No equality in the exported interface identifies these choices with a separately constructed tower presentation. `ABound` itself permits centers in a general ultrametric normed extension of $\mathbb Q_3$. To use the simple scalar residue comparison, retain the exhibited integral construction, or supply the corresponding distance certificates for an arbitrary coefficient field.
